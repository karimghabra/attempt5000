# Flow and context break the crossover ceiling

Primate vision has no dedicated T-, X- or Y-junction detector. It types a junction from how three distributed signals relate: **terminations** carried by end-stopped cells, **continuation** carried by a co-axial association field, and **co-occurring orientations** carried by a separate "plaid" population that fires while the ordinary oriented cells at that point are being suppressed. It leaves the hard calls (occlusion versus crossing, completion behind an obstacle, which fragment continues which) to slower stages that arrive 30–100 ms after the feedforward sweep, pool context over many receptive fields, and allow completion only when the interrupter is inferred to be a nearer layer. When one view is ambiguous, cortex does not look harder locally but brings in depth, motion and memory; where none of these exists, as at the non-reversing X-junction that two overlapping absorbers produce, depth order stays bistable. The vessel literature puts numbers on the user's diagnosis: local readers of crossings land at **41–67% accuracy**, the same band as vascnet's plateau, and recovering a tree from a 2-D projection is formally ill-posed, **NP-hard without edge directions but linear-time once each edge's direction is known**, which is exactly what stabilized flow video measures. Not all of the plateau is identifiability, though. About two-thirds of retinal crossings fragment into two degree-3 skeleton nodes, cross-orientation normalization like vascnet's can erase a thin vessel inside a thick one, and measuring arms inside the junction core roughly doubles orientation error; none of these failures depends on noise, and all can be fixed within one frame. The design programme therefore has two phases. The first repairs the single-frame representation. The second adds the cues only video has: flow direction measured off the overlap, lagged flux "fingerprints" shared by the two arms of one vessel, graph constraints driven by flow direction, and defocus used where cortex uses disparity. No published 2-D method uses flow to decide junction type, so the second phase is both the largest expected gain and genuinely new.

## Primate cortex types junctions from relations, not with dedicated detectors

### End-stopped cells supply polarity-tagged termination signals

End-stopped ("hypercomplex") neurons respond to bars of optimal length and to contours that terminate inside the receptive field. In macaque V1 and V2, **about half signal the direction of termination and about 60% are selective for termination type** (line end versus corner) ([Heider et al. 2000](https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1460-9568.2000.00293.x)).

Their end-zone inhibition is computed separately for each contrast sign. In end-stopped V1 cells it is "highly selective for the relative sign of contrast" between centre and end zones. The authors argue that this lets the cells separate occlusion from shadow or transparency configurations, "obviating the need for explicit junction detectors" ([Yazdanbakhsh & Livingstone 2006](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn1693)).

End-stopping is formally a curvature operator: the difference of two simple-cell responses scales with curvature, and end-stopped cells in cat are curvature-selective ([Dobbins, Zucker & Cynader 1987](https://doi.org/10.1038/329438a0)). The suppression is orientation-specific, strongest for iso-oriented stimuli at the ends ([Cavanaugh, Bair & Movshon 2002](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00693.2001)). That is why the stem of a T can drive its end-stopped cell even though the bar crosses its end zone.

Two details matter for vessels. **Spatial summation is 2.3 times longer at low contrast** ([Sceniak et al. 1999](https://doi.org/10.1038/11197)). A fixed-length end-stopping operator will therefore chop faint or defocused vessels into false line ends that a contrast-adaptive operator would leave intact. In addition, part of primate end-stopping comes from feedback from V2 and V3 rather than from V1 alone ([Nassi, Lomber & Born 2013](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.5124-12.2013)).

### A fixed association field matches vessel geometry within 2%

Collinear flankers outside the classical receptive field facilitate **42% of superficial complex cells in alert macaque V1** and improve human detection by 40%. Facilitation lies along the receptive-field ends and inhibition on the flanks ([Kapadia et al. 1995](https://doi.org/10.1016/0896-6273%2895%2990175-2); [Kapadia, Westheimer & Gilbert 2000](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.2000.84.4.2048)).

Psychophysically, the "association field" links nearby, co-oriented, co-circular elements whatever their contrast polarity. Objective tasks show essentially no linking beyond about 90° of turn ([Hess, May & Dumoulin 2014](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/18317/1/Hess_May_Dumoulin_2013.pdf); [Carrigan, Palmer & Kellman 2016](https://kellmanlab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/357/2025/09/carrigan_palmer_kellman_2016.pdf)).

These weights do not need to be learned. Edge co-occurrence statistics of natural images predict human grouping ([Geisler et al. 2001](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0042-6989%2800%2900277-7)). **Line co-occurrence statistics measured on retinal vessel centrelines match the V1 connectivity model with under 2% least-squares error, independent of dataset** ([Abbasi-Sureshjani et al. 2017](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00422-017-0718-x)). This is the strongest published justification for using fixed, cortex-derived continuation kernels on vessels.

### At a crossing, cortex suppresses oriented chains and labels the point separately

The way cortex handles a crossing is counter-intuitive: where two orientations co-occur, the oriented channels are mostly suppressed rather than linked. In Kapadia's experiments, **an orthogonal line placed between two collinear bars abolished both the perceptual and the neuronal facilitation** ([Kapadia et al. 1995](https://doi.org/10.1016/0896-6273%2895%2990175-2)).

Two-photon imaging of superficial macaque V1 found **cross-orientation inhibition in about 94% of orientation-tuned neurons**. It also found a separate population of non-oriented neurons that respond weakly to gratings but strongly to plaids. These neurons are selective for the angle between the two components, and about 95% of them are direction-insensitive ([Guan et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2020.01.005)).

Other V1 cells give "supra-optimal" responses when centre and surround orientations differ ([Sillito et al. 1995](https://doi.org/10.1038/378492a0)). Many superficial V1 neurons prefer corners, junctions and curvature to their oriented components ([Tang et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2017.11.039)).

The implied architecture is that a co-occurrence channel *labels* the crossing while both oriented chains are weakened at that point. The chains do not carry themselves through. A network that relies on oriented energy plus cross-orientation inhibition to keep a thin vessel alive inside a thick one is working against the circuit it imitates.

### V2 and V4 combine half-lines and curvature rather than storing junction templates

In superficial V2, **91 of 114 neurons were angle-selective and 36% preferred wide angles of 60–150°**. Yet their responses to the optimal angle were indistinguishable from their responses to the optimal single half-line, so these cells are "unlikely to be specific angle detectors" ([Ito & Komatsu 2004](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4364-03.2004)).

A cascade model explains them as a **linear sum of half-line ("ray") subunits**, sharpened by **suppression of non-preferred rays** and by **divisive gain control by the number of rays**. It contains no facilitatory AND between the preferred pair ([Ito & Goda 2011](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1460-9568.2010.07489.x)). About a third of V2 cells are selective for orientation combinations through inhibitory interactions between two positions, often with subregions about 90° apart ([Anzai, Peng & Van Essen 2007](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn1975)).

In V4, 64.7% of neurons prefer curves or corners to bars. They cluster into separate curve and corner domains whose main difference is smoothness ([Jiang et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.63798)), and they are tuned to curvature at a specific position relative to the object ([Pasupathy & Connor 2001](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.2001.86.5.2505)).

The research notes found **no single-unit study that directly contrasts T-, L-, X- and Y-junction selectivity** in any area. In humans, disrupting junctions leaves decoding in early visual cortex untouched while collapsing decoding in scene-selective areas ([Choo & Walther 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2016.04.021)). Explicit junction type is an intermediate-level readout from a population.

### Humans read junctions relationally, from wide context, and hesitate at shallow crossings

Junction *angles* predicted human scene-categorization errors better than junction *types* (r = 0.730 versus 0.513). Translating contours so that they no longer met cut accuracy from 53.8% to 31.9% ([Walther & Shen 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3984348)). Yet intersections are not basic visual-search features once size and line terminators are controlled ([Wolfe & DiMase 2003](https://doi.org/10.1068/p3414)).

Junctions in natural images need context several times larger than the junction itself. **Apertures 13 pixels wide misclassified 27% of patches when observers judged whether each was centred on an occlusion point, and errors did not reach their minimum until apertures were about 100 px**. Synthetic T-junctions, by contrast, were recognized at nearly every aperture size ([McDermott 2004](https://doi.org/10.1068/p5265)).

Tracing a path through a node-link graph took a median 6.81 s with no crossings, 14.74 s with near-90° crossings and **29.41 s with small-angle crossings**. At the small-angle crossings, eye movements shuttled back and forth because "the viewer was uncertain about which way to go" ([Huang 2008](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0810.4431)). Humans hesitate at exactly vascnet's hardest geometry.

When monkeys trace a curve, V1 responses along the target are enhanced "even if the two curves crossed each other" ([Roelfsema, Lamme & Spekreijse 1998](https://doi.org/10.1038/26475)). The enhancement spreads at about 50 ms per receptive field and slows where curves lie close together ([Pooresmaeili & Roelfsema 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2014.10.007)). Models implement this spread with modulatory horizontal connections that act only where feedforward drive already exists, so the label cannot leak into blank image ([Brosch, Neumann & Roelfsema 2015](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1004489)).

### Cortical timing doubles as an architecture specification

In V1 the feedforward response starts about 40 ms after stimulus onset. Boundary signals appear at about 70 ms in superficial layers, and feedback-driven filling-in arrives about 30 ms later through the feedback-recipient layers ([Self et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2013.09.013)).

Horizontal activity spreads at only 0.1–0.25 m/s ([Grinvald et al. 1994](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.14-05-02545.1994)). Feedforward and feedback axons between V1 and V2 conduct at about 3.5 m/s ([Girard, Hupé & Bullier 2001](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.2001.85.3.1328)). This difference is why long-range context is attributed to feedback from larger receptive fields. For vascnet, lateral kernels should iterate only over short distances, and long-range junction context should arrive in a single pooling step at a coarser level.

The X row's silent end-stop cells are the one entry stated explicitly in a source ([Weidenbacher & Neumann 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2691604/)).

## Completion behind an obstacle is gated by an inferred nearer layer and arrives late

### A junction triggers completion; relatability and support ratio set its strength

In displays matched in every occlusion cue except junction structure, removing the L- and T-junctions, or making them locally inconsistent, eliminated amodal completion. This happened even though relatability and surface similarity were unchanged ([Rubin 2001](https://doi.org/10.1068/p3173)). A junction is the trigger.

Which fragments connect is then well described by relatability: their extensions must meet via a smooth, monotonic curve bending no more than 90° ([Kellman & Shipley 1991](https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0285%2891%2990009-D)). Strength is graded by the **support ratio** of visible edge length to total edge length, which makes the rule scale-invariant ([Shipley & Kellman 1992](https://doi.org/10.3758/bf03206762)). It is also graded by a preference for continuations without inflections ([Singh & Hoffman 1999](https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03206908); [Takeichi et al. 1995](https://doi.org/10.1068/p240373)). Integration across gaps as large as 13° is approximately scale-invariant ([Ringach & Shapley 1996](https://doi.org/10.1016/0042-6989%2896%2900062-4)).

Fixed-weight implementations exist. Stochastic completion fields compute the probability that a random-walk contour passes through each position and orientation. The field is the product of a source field and a sink field, and the most likely path is an elastica ([Williams & Jacobs 1997](https://doi.org/10.1162/neco.1997.9.4.837)). The 2.1-D sketch orders overlapping regions by minimizing disrupted edges plus elastica continuation costs ([Nitzberg & Mumford 1990](https://mlanthology.org/iccv/1990/nitzberg1990iccv-d)).

Relatability is contested as a complete theory: it is "neither necessary nor sufficient" when volumes rather than contours merge ([Tse 1999](https://doi.org/10.1006/cogp.1999.0715); [Anderson 2007](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.114.2.470)). Constant-width tubes, however, are close to the case it was built for.

### Neurons complete a line only when the interrupter is coded as nearer

Some V1 neurons stopped responding to a bar when a small patch interrupted it. They stayed silent when the patch had uncrossed disparity, meaning it appeared behind the bar, and **resumed responding only when the patch appeared in front** ([Sugita 1999](https://doi.org/10.1038/45785)). In V2, collinear facilitation across an orthogonal line was blocked when the line simply crossed the contour. It **recovered when the line was placed in the near depth plane** ([Bakin, Nakayama & Gilbert 2000](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.20-21-08188.2000)).

The rule is that a line interrupted by another line is completed only if the interrupter is assigned to a nearer layer. A plain 2-D crossing blocks completion.

V4 adds the complementary step. Responses to a contour are suppressed when a T-junction makes it "accidental", that is, created by the occluder. **A small gap that breaks the abutment reverses the suppression** ([Bushnell et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4766-10.2011)). This is Nakayama's intrinsic/extrinsic distinction: a border belongs to the nearer region, extrinsic borders link to other extrinsic borders, and extrinsic terminators are excluded from motion integration ([Nakayama, Shimojo & Silverman 1989](https://doi.org/10.1068/p180055); [Shimojo, Silverman & Nakayama 1989](https://doi.org/10.1016/0042-6989%2889%2990047-3)).

V2 also builds contours out of aligned line ends. **About 44% of orientation-selective V2 neurons signal a contour defined only by aligned line ends, against 1 of 60 in V1**. The authors explain this by pooling end-stopped responses oriented orthogonal to the contour ([von der Heydt & Peterhans 1989](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.09-05-01731.1989)). A further 32% of V2 neurons bridge gaps of 1–3.5°, and thin lines that close the gaps abolish the response ([Peterhans & von der Heydt 1989](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.09-05-01749.1989)).

### Border ownership is fast and common but nearly absent from local cues alone

Border ownership is the question of which side owns an edge, and therefore of depth order at an occlusion. Side-of-figure modulation appears in **more than 50% of V2 and V4 neurons and 18% of top-layer V1 neurons, under 25 ms after response onset** ([Zhou, Friedman & von der Heydt 2000](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.20-17-06594.2000)). Local polarity and stereo edges are signalled within 13 ms of edge onset, while the contextual ownership signal lags by about 30 ms ([Sugihara, Qiu & von der Heydt 2011](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00928.2010)). In natural scenes, **local patches alone produced about 2% of the full ownership signal, and distant context about 35%** ([Williford & von der Heydt 2016](https://doi.org/10.1523/ENEURO.0127-16.2016)).

How that context arrives is unresolved. One account uses feedback from annular "grouping cells" ([Craft et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00203.2007)). The other uses interactions within V2, in which a T's top and stem facilitate each other only if the top's ownership is consistent with occlusion ([Zhaoping 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2005.04.005)).

The best descendant model that runs on images reaches 71.5% figure-ground accuracy against 83.9% for humans. It keeps only the strongest orientation per pixel ([Hu, von der Heydt & Niebur 2019](https://www.eneuro.org/content/eneuro/6/3/ENEURO.0479-18.2019.full.pdf)), which discards exactly the second orientation that defines a crossing.

### Completion is slow and grows with the hidden region, which explains the additive-bipole failure

Illusory-contour signals appear in V2 superficial layers at about 70 ms and in V1 at about 100 ms ([Lee & Nguyen 2001](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.98.4.1907)). In human EEG, amodal and modal completion share a stage at about 140 ms and diverge at about 240 ms ([Murray et al. 2004](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1996-04.2004)). Ventral-stream responses to partial objects lag those to whole objects by about 100 ms ([Tang et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2014.06.017)). **Completion time increases with the size of the occluded region** ([Shore & Enns 1997](https://doi.org/10.1037//0096-1523.23.4.980)).

Higher areas show the same pattern. V4 shows a second, occlusion-selective response peak after prefrontal activity begins ([Fyall et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.25784)). IT encodes occluder and occluded as separately weighted, linearly summed components ([Namima & Pasupathy 2021](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2992-20.2021)).

For a fixed network, this reads as a first "mosaic" pass followed by a few gated recurrent completion iterations, with the number of iterations scaled to the width of the crossing vessel. It also explains vascnet's ablation result. Additive bipole completion is ungated, early completion, which is the variant biology avoids. Grossberg's analysis shows that the same bipole machinery can break the weaker branch of an unequal X into a pseudo-T "end gap" ([Grossberg 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4726768/)).

### Overlapping absorbers form transparent X-junctions whose depth order is unrecoverable

At an X-junction, the ordinal pattern of the four luminances decides the percept. Non-reversing junctions support transparency with an ambiguous depth order. Single-reversing junctions support transparency with a unique order. Double-reversing junctions do not support transparency at all ([Adelson & Anandan 1990](https://persci.mit.edu/pub_abstracts/ordinal90_abs.html)). Perceived transmittance follows the ratio of Michelson contrasts inside and outside the layer ([Singh & Anderson 2002](https://ruccs.sas.rutgers.edu/images/archive/personal-manish-singh/papers/singh_anderson_2002a.pdf)), and the lower-contrast side of a junction that preserves polarity is split into two layers ([Anderson 1997](https://doi.org/10.1068/p260419)).

Under Beer–Lambert absorption, two dark absorbers on a bright field multiply transmittances. The overlap is therefore darker than either vessel, and polarity is preserved along both, which makes it a non-reversing X. The notes draw the consequence that matters most for the design: because log-attenuations add and addition commutes, **which vessel is on top is not recoverable from a single projection's intensities, while which segment continues which is well posed**.

Cortex couples photometry to motion in exactly this regime. When the intersections of a plaid have luminances consistent with one grating lying transparently over the other, coherent pattern motion gives way to two independent motions ([Stoner, Albright & Ramachandran 1990](https://doi.org/10.1038/344153a0)), and MT's sensitivity to the component motions follows the percept ([Stoner & Albright 1992](https://doi.org/10.1038/358412a0)). The practical upshot for vascnet is to stop treating depth order as a target. Instead, treat "two layers are present here", whether shown by photometry, blur or flow, as the gate that licenses completion across a vessel.

## Motion, common fate and defocus supply the cues a single frame lacks

### Two velocities at one pixel mark a crossing

Overlapping motions are represented separately early and pruned later. V1 cells keep responding to their preferred component under motion transparency, while MT responses are suppressed ([Snowden et al. 1991](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.11-09-02768.1991)). Transparency is perceived only when opposing local motion signals are spatially unbalanced. Locally balanced displays become transparent again when the components differ in **stereo depth or spatial-frequency content** ([Qian, Andersen & Adelson 1994](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.14-12-07357.1994); [Qian & Andersen 1994](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.14-12-07367.1994)), because MT's opponent inhibition operates mainly between signals of similar disparity ([Bradley, Qian & Andersen 1995](https://doi.org/10.1038/373609a0)). The number of perceived components is read from the shape of the MT population response, not from separate peaks ([Treue, Hol & Rauber 2000](https://doi.org/10.1038/72985)).

The limits are quantitative. Two directions are represented independently once they differ by **more than about 20°** ([Braddick, Wishart & Curran 2002](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0042-6989%2802%2900018-4)). Direction alone supports at most two surfaces ([Edwards & Greenwood 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.visres.2005.01.026)), and adding speed differences raises this to three ([Greenwood & Edwards 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.visres.2005.07.020)). Segmenting by speed alone needs large speed differences and about 200 ms ([Masson, Mestre & Stone 1999](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0042-6989%2899%2900143-1)). Locally paired opposing dots collapse into a single vector-average motion ([Curran & Braddick 2000](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0042-6989%2800%2900084-5)). MT tuning starts at the vector average and resolves into the components over 70–100 ms ([Xiao & Huang 2015](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2175-15.2015)). Transparent motion also biases MT speed coding ([Krekelberg & van Wezel 2013](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00333.2013)).

For vessels the mapping is direct, but per the notes it is untested. An overlap carrying two velocity vectors at the same pixels is a crossing or a contact. A branch point carries one velocity per pixel, which changes direction across space. Dense red-cell columns crossing at small angles risk the vector-average artifact. Speeds used to link arms should be measured on the arms, not inside the overlap.

The fixed-weight machinery is standard. Spatiotemporal quadrature filters are squared, summed into motion energy and combined opponently ([Adelson & Bergen 1985](https://doi.org/10.1364/josaa.2.000284)). They feed MT-like units that take weighted sums followed by rectification and divisive normalization ([Simoncelli & Heeger 1998](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0042-6989%2897%2900183-1); [Rust et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn1786)). Speed tuning becomes less dependent on spatial frequency when several frequencies move together ([Priebe, Cassanello & Lisberger 2003](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.23-13-05650.2003)), which favours robustness to the high-frequency loss of defocus.

Brain-like speed decoders are, however, biased by contrast. **A 70%-contrast grating had to be slowed by 35% to match a 10%-contrast grating** ([Stone & Thompson 1992](https://doi.org/10.1016/0042-6989%2892%2990209-2)). MT preferred speeds shift lower at low contrast ([Krekelberg, van Wezel & Albright 2006](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1983-06.2006)), and a prior favouring slow speeds explains the bias ([Weiss, Simoncelli & Adelson 2002](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn0602-858)). A defocused, lower-contrast vessel's flow will therefore be under-estimated unless responses are contrast-normalized. This argues for weighting flow *direction* above flow *speed* when one arm is out of focus.

### Shared temporal structure can bind the two arms of one vessel

Human vision groups whatever changes together. Regions whose elements reverse direction in synchrony segregate as a form ([Lee & Blake 1999](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.284.5417.1165)), and correlated contrast modulations bind gratings into one object ([Alais, Blake & Lee 1998](https://doi.org/10.1038/414)). Elements that brighten or darken together group strongly even without fixed luminance differences ([Sekuler & Bennett 2001](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9280.00382)). Temporal structure is extracted regardless of which feature carries it ([Guttman, Gilroy & Blake 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.visres.2004.10.014)), and phase delays near 13 ms suffice for figure-ground segregation in young observers ([Kandil & Fahle 2001](https://doi.org/10.1046/j.0953-816x.2001.01573.x)). Crucially, **temporal synchrony and good continuation combine better than probability summation predicts** ([Lee & Blake 2001](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0042-6989%2801%2900086-4)), which argues for multiplying geometric and temporal pairing evidence rather than averaging them.

Whether synchrony itself drives grouping is disputed: the effects can be explained by ordinary early transient mechanisms ([Farid & Adelson 2001](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn0901-875)). The dispute actually favours a fixed-weight design, since temporal band-pass filtering followed by correlation would then suffice. Common motion of the visible ends is enough for 4-month-old infants to see a rod occluded at its centre as one object, for any rigid translation ([Kellman, Spelke & Short 1986](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3948595/)).

The vessel analogue in the notes is an inference. Red-cell flux fluctuations (plasma gaps, cell clusters, pulsatility) travel downstream. The two arms of one vessel on either side of a crossing should therefore share a temporal signature, offset by path length divided by velocity. The crossing vessel carries an independent signature, and a parent shares its signature with both daughters. The psychophysics found used only zero-lag synchrony; grouping by lagged correlation is untested.

### Streaming, accretion and memory decide continuity through occlusion

The stream/bounce display is the closest laboratory model of crossing versus touching. Two identical targets that converge, overlap and separate are seen streaming through each other unless a transient marks the coincidence. A brief sound biases perception toward bouncing. With a tone, bounce reports survived vertical offsets of up to 17.9 arcmin that make streaming physically consistent ([Grove & Sakurai 2009](https://doi.org/10.1068/p5860)).

Constant velocity favours streaming, and a small amount of contradictory depth information reverses the bias abruptly, whereas contradictory pattern information acts only gradually ([Bertenthal, Banton & Bradbury 1993](https://doi.org/10.1068/p220193)). X-junction cues and local motion coherence promote streaming ([Matsuno & Tomonaga 2011](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13414-011-0126-6)). Continuous occluded motion with accretion and deletion pushes toward streaming even against a sound ([Kawachi & Gyoba 2013](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13414-012-0419-4)). Correspondence based on surface features needs about 200 ms after the coincidence ([Kawachi, Kawabe & Gyoba 2011](https://doi.org/10.1068/i0399)).

Streaming maps onto a vessel crossing and bouncing onto a tangential contact, the case vascnet finds hardest. The cues that tip humans (trajectory continuity, constant velocity, a depth difference) all have analogues in flow video.

Object continuity rests mainly on spatiotemporal criteria. Multiple-object tracking survives brief full occlusion **only if objects show accretion and deletion along fixed contours**, and visible occluders are not required ([Scholl & Pylyshyn 1999](https://doi.org/10.1006/cogp.1998.0698)). A consistent re-emergence makes observers "irresistibly perceive a persisting object, even when the pre- and postocclusion views contrast featurally" ([Flombaum & Scholl 2006](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-1523.32.4.840)). Attentive tracking links views by proximity rather than extrapolation ([Keane & Pylyshyn 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cogpsych.2005.12.001); [Franconeri, Pylyshyn & Scholl 2012](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13414-011-0265-9)).

Whether surface features also count is contested ([Mitroff & Alvarez 2007](https://doi.org/10.3758/bf03193113) versus [Hollingworth & Franconeri 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2009.08.004)). A formal compromise defines an object as the path through feature space that minimizes feature change, with straight paths winning when features are equal ([Feldman & Tremoulet 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2004.12.008)).

Oculomotor systems do extrapolate. Pursuit keeps a residual velocity of about 60% of target speed through a disappearance ([Becker & Fuchs 1985](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00237843)), and a velocity memory stays engaged when a physical occluder explains the disappearance ([Churchland, Chou & Lisberger 2003](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.01056.2002)).

Kinetic occlusion orders depth. The surface whose texture is deleted and accreted at an edge is behind ([Granrud et al. 1984](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6488967/)). Motion into a boundary reads as "behind" and shearing motion as "in front" ([Royden, Baker & Allman 1988](https://doi.org/10.1068/p170289)). MT responds better to terminators intrinsic to the object than to accidents of occlusion ([Pack, Gartland & Born 2004](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4387-03.2004)).

**V2 ownership signals persist for a second or more after cues are removed, update rapidly on new information, and decay slowly when the display turns ambiguous** ([O'Herron & von der Heydt 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2009.01.014)). That asymmetric hysteresis is a rule vascnet's type voting can copy.

One caution from the notes: in transmission imaging, a hidden vessel's red cells are not deleted at a crossing. Their attenuation adds to the overlap. The transparency analogue of accretion and deletion is therefore a transient extra darkening that travels through the overlap at the hidden vessel's speed.

### Defocus can stand in for disparity, but cannot give its sign

Blur differences alone can order depth through the occlusion border. If the border is blurred, the blurrier surface is nearer; if it is sharp, the sharper surface is nearer ([Marshall et al. 1996](https://doi.org/10.1364/josaa.13.000681); [Zannoli et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1167/16.6.17)). Humans use this cue coarsely, though. **Blur-discrimination Weber fractions are 0.28–0.56** ([Mather & Smith 2002](https://doi.org/10.1068/p3254)), and blur is weighted far below disparity ([Mather & Smith 2000](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0042-6989%2800%2900178-4)). Observers fail when blur is rendered on a single display plane, and chromatic aberration helps them decide whether defocus is nearer or farther than focus ([Zannoli et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1167/16.6.17)).

Optimal defocus estimation from individual natural patches is precise with simple spatial receptive fields. **Chromatic aberration fully resolves the near/far sign ambiguity** ([Burge & Geisler 2011](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1108491108)), but a monochrome microscope camera lacks that cue. V4 neurons are jointly tuned to shape and boundary blur, with blur acting as a gain on shape selectivity ([Oleskiw, Nowack & Pasupathy 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-017-02438-8)). Blur discrimination needs no dedicated blur units, only visible contrast energy ([Watson & Ahumada 2011](https://doi.org/10.1167/11.5.10)). Blur similarity groups an edge with a region and makes that region look nearer ([Palmer & Brooks 2008](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0012729)).

Blur and disparity follow the same geometry, and blur is more precise away from fixation ([Held, Cooper & Banks 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2012.01.033)). On that basis the notes propose, speculatively, that defocus can take over disparity's junction roles in monocular video: depth-specific opponent suppression, layer gating of completion, classifying terminators as intrinsic or extrinsic, and the abrupt stream/bounce flip.

Without a focus sweep or asymmetric optics, defocus cannot give the *sign*, so the depth order between a sharp and a defocused vessel stays unknown. It can give two things. One is strong evidence that two layers are present. The other is a continuity cue: a vessel at roughly constant depth keeps roughly constant blur along its length, while branch daughters share their parent's depth at the branch point.

## Vessel benchmarks put local crossover readers at 41–67%

### Independent systems land in vascnet's band

When crossover handling is measured directly, independent systems land in vascnet's band. Graph label propagation whose junction weights used "just angular property of neighbouring filaments" scored **0.505 crossover accuracy on DRIVE and 0.426 on STARE**, and 0.536–0.546 on 2-D neuronal images ([De et al. 2016](https://www.ece.ualberta.ca/~lcheng5/papers/DeEtAl_TMI16.pdf)).

A two-stage CNN's confusion matrices give **crossing recall of about 0.67, 0.49 and 0.51 against three DRIVE graders, and 0.41 on unseen IOSTAR images, against bifurcation recall of about 0.80–0.87** ([Pratt et al. 2018](https://cgi.csc.liv.ac.uk/~frans/PostScriptFiles/joiPratt_2017.pdf)). That is the same asymmetry vascnet shows. A learned multi-task detector reports that wide crossing vessels cause errors and makes no attempt to type junctions ([Uslu & Bharath 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1806.03175)).

Base rates matter for reading any of these numbers. Crossings are about 19% of ophthalmologist-labelled junctions in DRIVE (520 of 2,687) and 24% in STARE ([Hamad, Tegolo & Valenti 2014](https://ias-iss.org/ojs/IAS/article/viewFile/1101/952)). A classifier that always answers "bifurcation" therefore already scores 76–81% type accuracy, so crossing recall or balanced accuracy is the metric to track.

### Projection makes tree recovery ill-posed, unless edge directions are known

Theory explains why lowering noise does not help. Recovering tree topology from the planar graph that a 3-D tree projects to is ill-posed. Under a generative growth model it is **NP-hard when edge directions are unknown but linear-time when each edge's direction toward or away from the root is known** ([Estrada et al., TPAMI 2015](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1109/TPAMI.2014.2382116)).

Empirically, a local orientation search gave artery/vein accuracies of 0.537–0.793. Adding tree-level priors raised them to **0.909–0.935**; the priors were that vessels usually continue rather than split, that arteries cross veins, and colour ([Estrada et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4685460)). Hard constraints such as "arteries cross only veins", together with a dominant-flow-orientation term, carry similar weight in other systems ([Hu, Abràmoff & Garvin 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4652785)).

In stabilized flow video, edge direction is a measurement, not a prior to be guessed. That places the user's data on the tractable side of Estrada's divide. The notes found **no published 2-D method that uses flow to decide crossing versus bifurcation at individual junctions**. Flow has been used for velocity, artery/vein timing and network reconstruction ([Fraser, Goldman & Ellis 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3393846)), and vessel superposition appears in the intravital literature as an artefact rather than a cue ([Frontiers in Physiology 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2022.886675/pdf)).

### The best single-frame systems show the plateau is not all identifiability

Tracking in invertible orientation scores typed **109 of 114 crossings (95.6%) correctly on HRF fundus images, but only 76.1% of bifurcations, and 18.6% of the "bifurcations" were actually parts of crossings** ([Bekkers et al. 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.3530)). The orientation-score representation preserves crossings; the residual confusion sits at bifurcations near crossings.

Skeleton rules applied to manual segmentations reached crossing recall/precision of 86.8/87.6% on DRIVE and 88.4/93.3% on STARE. The decisive structural fact is that **326 of 518 detected DRIVE crossings (63%) and 252 of 349 STARE crossings (72%) appeared as two degree-3 nodes joined by a short edge rather than as one degree-4 node**. Misclassified crossings default to bifurcations. A connectivity rule requires each through-pair to continue onward ([Hamad, Tegolo & Valenti 2014](https://ias-iss.org/ojs/IAS/article/viewFile/1101/952)).

Excluding the junction core and refining position from a model of the arms cut **orientation error from 7.2° to 3.8° and location error from 2.04 to 1.1 px** ([Tsai et al. 2004](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109/TITB.2004.826733)). A Bayesian model of joint configurations reached 95.2% accuracy on 3,435 DRIVE test joints ([Qureshi, Hunter & Al-Diri 2014](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2014/papers/Qureshi_A_Bayesian_Framework_2014_CVPR_paper.pdf)).

A kernel over position, orientation, curvature and intensity grouped 76% of 274 hard patches correctly. It separated crossings, which keep low curvature through the junction, from branches, which show high curvature. It failed where a branch left its parent nearly orthogonally ([Abbasi-Sureshjani et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.08049)).

COSFIRE detectors configured from prototypes find junctions on binary maps at 98.5% recall and 96.1% precision, but they pool bifurcations and crossings together ([Azzopardi & Petkov 2013](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/8375/1/PAMI2013.pdf)). An inhibition-augmented variant uses crossing prototypes to veto the bifurcation response ([Azzopardi publication list](https://www.cs.rug.nl/~george/my-publications/?tgid=44)).

These systems differ in inputs and labels, so the comparison shows direction only. Junction labels are themselves noisy: they come from single annotators, close bifurcations get merged ([Lyu, Cheng & Zhang 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9273761/)), and cross-grader classification accuracies sit near 0.70–0.80 ([Pratt et al. 2018](https://cgi.csc.liv.ac.uk/~frans/PostScriptFiles/joiPratt_2017.pdf)).

### Four noise-independent mechanisms in vascnet could produce the same plateau

Read against vascnet's design, four failure mechanisms that do not depend on noise are plausible alongside true identifiability, and each would produce the observed plateau. The first is **normalization that suppresses the thin vessel**. vascnet's cross-orientation inhibition and divisive normalization, followed by non-maximum suppression in orientation space, reproduce the V1 behaviour that suppresses a weak orientation where a strong one co-occurs ([Guan et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2020.01.005)). A thin vessel inside a thick one then loses its centreline over the overlap and reappears as two stems abutting the thick vessel (the Grossberg end gap), which every downstream rule reads as two T-branches.

The second is **split nodes that drift beyond a fixed fusion radius**. Simple geometry, an inference rather than a published result, says the thin vessel's path through a thick vessel of width W at crossing angle α is about W/sin α long: roughly 2.9 W at 20° and 3.9 W at 15°. The two degree-3 nodes Hamad describes therefore drift apart as the angle grows shallower and escape any fixed fusion radius. The third is **orientation sampling that is too coarse**. By the notes' sampling argument, also an inference, leakage between orientation layers is controlled by orientation spacing, so the number of orientations must exceed π divided by the smallest crossing angle, with a safety factor. Sixteen orientations give 11.25° spacing, so crossings below roughly 20–25° leak between layers. The fourth is **arm measurement inside the junction core**, exactly where Tsai's exclusion halved orientation error.

Tangential contacts, by contrast, are genuine single-frame metamers: the same pixels arise from a shallow crossing and from a kiss. Part of the crossing-on-branch-point family is metameric too. How the plateau splits between these causes can only be settled experimentally, which is why that experiment comes first in the build order below.

### Single-frame repairs keep both vessels alive through the junction

**Module 1** replaces orientation non-maximum suppression inside a junction neighbourhood with the full orientation column. It splits energy into single-sided directional parts, so each arm is a ray leaving the vertex rather than an undirected line. Junctions are then read as peaks in the directional column, following Bekkers' rule: two opposite peaks mark a straight vessel, three a branch, and four in opposite pairs a crossing. Raising the orientation count locally to 32–36 should push the leakage angle toward 10°.

Two shortcuts should be avoided at junctions. Second-order structure-tensor and tensor-voting summaries hold only one dominant orientation plus an isotropic "ball" part ([Medioni, Lee & Tang 2000](https://www.cse.unr.edu/~bebis/CS773C/ObjectRecognition/Papers/Medioni00.pdf)), so the two tangents of a crossing average away. Circular variance flags points with several orientations but cannot say whether the extra orientation continues or terminates ([Hansen & Neumann 2004](https://doi.org/10.1162/089976604773135087)).

**Module 2** is the main physiology-backed change to vascnet's horizontal stage, and it has three parts. The first is an explicit co-occurrence channel, X(x), that sums products of bipole-supported energies at different orientations (Weidenbacher's X map); where X is high, the weight of the cross-orientation divisive pool is reduced, so each orientation is normalized mostly by itself. The second pools normalization within a scale band, so a thin, sharp vessel in a fine band is not divided by the coarse-band energy of a thick or defocused vessel, mirroring MT's opponency, which is specific to disparity and spatial frequency. The third makes end zones and association gain contrast-adaptive: end zones lengthen at low contrast ([Sceniak et al. 1999](https://doi.org/10.1038/11197)), and collinear gain rises for faint segments, following the cat finding that facilitation is strongest near threshold ([Polat et al. 1998](https://doi.org/10.1038/35372)). The gating itself is a proposal in the notes, not a measured circuit. The prediction is falsifiable: thin-vessel centreline recall inside overlaps should rise sharply at width ratios below 0.3, without new false crossings elsewhere.

**Module 3** moves all arm measurement onto rings outside the junction core. The inner radius is at least half the thick vessel's width plus a margin. Further rings extend to several widths, in line with McDermott's context requirement and the annular grouping-cell geometry. Each ray records angle, intrinsic width, integrated optical density (OD), blur and, later, flow. Rays are combined the way V2 is described: a linear sum, suppression of non-preferred rays, and divisive gain by ray count. Pairing then uses vascnet's existing straightness margin, an elastica or relatability cost, and least-squares relocation. Tsai's halving of orientation error is the benchmark this reader should reproduce.

**Module 5** replaces vascnet's fixed fusion radius with one proportional to W_thick / sin α̂, where α̂ is the estimated angle between the thick vessel and the candidate through-path and the constant is fitted empirically. The fused node is then classified as one compound hypothesis: X; X plus branch (five arms, which Hamad's rules already handle); two Y's; or contact. Crowding supports the principle that interference zones scale with local scale ([Kurzawski et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10408772/)). The specific W/sin α form is geometry that needs calibration.

**Module 4** is the decision stage above modules 3 and 5. It computes Weidenbacher-style product maps: X as bipole × bipole, T as bipole × end-stop, Y as a triple product of end-stops in three directions, plus explicit maps for X-plus-branch and for contact. The hypotheses then compete push-pull, so crossing evidence suppresses bifurcation evidence at the same site and vice versa. A smoothness test keeps tortuous bends out of the junction pool. Weidenbacher reported only detection ROC curves for these maps, not per-class accuracy, hence tier B/C.

**Module 6** reintroduces completion in the form biology uses. A stochastic completion field between candidate arm pairs runs within orientation layers. It is *multiplied* into the feedforward evidence, using Weidenbacher's input × (1 + feedback) form, rather than added. It is allowed only where modules 7, 9 or 14 provide evidence that the interrupter is a separate layer. Strength is graded by support ratio, and the iteration count scales with the thick vessel's width, following Shore and Enns. Bushnell's abutment rule applies: an arm ending flush on a wall is a completion candidate, while an arm ending a few pixels short is a true termination.

**Module 7** upgrades vascnet's OD-additivity rule. It computes the log-additivity residual on the temporal-mean image, because red-cell granularity inside the thick vessel is structured clutter that a sensor-noise sweep never removes but averaging over frames does (an inference). It normalizes that residual by the thin vessel's own arm OD, which is the expected increment, and it uses integrated cross-sectional OD rather than peak OD, which for weak absorbers is approximately preserved under defocus (a physics inference). It vetoes double-reversing patterns, which signal light reflexes and artifacts. In its stronger variant it fits the overlap patch with both a two-tube additive OD model and a one-branching-tube model and compares the residuals, the log-domain analogue of layered representations with alpha masks.

### Flow video supplies direction, pairing and layer evidence

**Module 8** is the foundation for everything temporal. It uses quadrature filters in x, y and t within each scale band, opponent motion energy, MT-like divisive normalization, and speed pooled across spatial frequency. For each arm it reports flow direction, speed and confidence, measured outside the ring from module 3. Direction is weighted above speed wherever an arm is defocused, because of the contrast bias documented above.

**Module 9** looks inside the overlap. Opponent suppression acts only within a frequency or defocus band and within an orientation layer, the defocus analogue of MT's disparity-specific opponency. The module then reads the number of velocity modes from the shape of the population response. Two modes indicate a crossing or a contact, one mode that changes direction across space indicates a branch, and a single mode lying *between* the two vessel axes is flagged as a suspected vector-average artifact at a shallow crossing. Each component is assigned to the vessel whose axis it parallels. Whether motion along a contour is perceptually privileged is disputed ([Ledgeway & Hess 2002](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0042-6989%2801%2900247-4) versus [Bex, Simmers & Dakin 2003](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0042-6989%2803%2900329-8)), but inside a vessel the image statistics alone justify the coupling. The human 20° figure does not transfer directly. The network's own knee will depend on filter bandwidth and red-cell density, and that knee is the module's key measurement.

**Module 10** is the most promising single addition, and it is tier C because nothing like it has been tested at junctions. It takes a kymograph (space-time diagram) along each arm's centreline outside the ring, the same representation that sidestream dark-field analysis already uses to read red-cell velocity from streak orientation. It band-passes the kymograph in time and removes common-mode signals, since cardiac pulsatility and residual stabilization motion would otherwise correlate every vessel with every other (an inference). It then cross-correlates every arm pair over lags. A crossing should show two separate, strongly correlated pairs on opposite sides, with lags close to path length divided by speed. A contact should pair arms on the same side, a bifurcation should show a parent correlated with both daughters, and a crossing on a branch point should show one through-pair plus one parent–daughter triple. The correlation score is multiplied with the geometric continuation score, following Lee and Blake's synergy result. Because it uses temporal signature rather than direction, the cue should survive as the crossing angle goes to zero. It should also work on defocused arms where individual cells are not resolved, since grouping by common luminance change needs no resolved motion. The decisive falsifiable prediction is that module 10's accuracy stays flat across crossing angle while module 9's falls below its knee.

**Modules 11 to 13** are cheaper or narrower.

Module 11 checks flux. At a crossing, flux is conserved within each through-pair. At a bifurcation, parent flux equals the sum of the daughters' flux. A thick vessel's calibre and flux are unchanged across a crossing but reduced across a true branch, which turns Murray's law into a temporal test.

Module 12 projects temporal OD changes inside the overlap onto each candidate axis. It looks for the thin vessel's fluctuations crossing the thick vessel's footprint at the thin vessel's own speed. This is the most direct attack on the thin-over-thick case. Where a vessel behaves opaquely, the module looks instead for accretion and deletion at the other vessel's walls.

Module 13 wires direction-tuned units to excite units displaced along their preferred direction in the next frame. This enhances particle paths through the overlap and gives faint vessels flow-aligned orientation energy from motion streaks.

**Module 14** turns defocus from a nuisance into a cue. It estimates blur per arm from the ratio of fine-band to coarse-band energy, normalized by contrast, and estimates intrinsic width jointly with blur. Blur broadens profiles just as width does, so vascnet's width-similarity and Murray tests are biased across focused and defocused arms until that correction is made (an inference). Blur continuity then becomes a pairing term, and blur discontinuity becomes the "two layers" gate for module 6 and the band separator for modules 2 and 9. Border blur is used to order depth only where an overlap looks opaque; elsewhere depth order is reported as unknown, because a monochrome camera cannot resolve the sign of defocus. The known risk is a branch that dives in depth, so its blur changes along its length. The test set should include such branches.

### Global constraints and memory turn local votes into a consistent graph

**Module 15** gives each segment a flow direction from module 8 and uses it to constrain junction labels. Each through-pair at a crossing must conserve direction. A diverging bifurcation has one inflow and two outflows, and a converging one has two inflows and one outflow. A contact pairs arms on the same side, and any pairing that would require flow to reverse is forbidden. These constraints are propagated along the graph in the manner of line-labelling arc consistency. The surviving hypotheses are scored with Estrada-style priors (a vessel usually continues rather than splits) and with Hamad's onward-connectivity rule. Stagnant or oscillating capillaries give low-confidence directions, and those should relax the constraints rather than enforce them.

**Module 16** replaces majority type voting with asymmetric accumulation. Decisive events, such as a resolved cell passing straight through or a strong lagged correlation, update the label quickly. Ambiguous frames decay it slowly, mirroring V2 ownership signals. Windows should be counted in multiples of the transit time across the overlap plus the decorrelation time of the flux fluctuations, not in human milliseconds.

**Module 17** propagates vessel identity within orientation layers through modulatory links. Its angular tolerance is the single parameter that separates following a branch from leaking at a crossing; the notes suggest ±30–45° as a starting design value. Its step size is proportional to clearance. Its output feeds modules 10 and 15.

### Diagnose the plateau, repair the frame, then add flow

Before building, three diagnostics should apportion the plateau; they are this report's suggestion, not published practice. An **oracle-graph test** feeds the ground-truth centreline graph to vascnet's junction reader: if crossover typing jumps above 90%, the error lives in graph construction, which modules 1, 2, 3 and 5 address. A **metamer test** renders, for each typing failure, the alternative topology with the same generator and compares the image difference with the noise floor; failures on metamers are identifiability errors, while failures on non-metamers can be fixed in a single frame. A **factorial benchmark** crosses width ratio, crossing angle, branch-point offset in vessel widths, defocus difference, flow speed and the amplitude of red-cell flux fluctuations, and adds explicit contact geometries. It should report crossing recall and balanced accuracy in every cell, because the class prior makes overall accuracy misleading.

| Priority | Build | Modules | Why first | Signature of success |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Compound-junction reader | 3, 5, 4 | Tier-A precedent (split crossings, exclusion regions, E1 errors); single-frame and cheap | Split-crossing recovery rises, concentrated at shallow angles, with no new false fusions at twin bifurcations |
| 2 | Orientation-layer core with gated normalization | 1, 2 | Targets thin-vessel erasure; backed by V1 physiology | Thin-vessel continuity through overlaps; crossing recall at width ratio ≤0.3 |
| 3 | Kymograph arm correlator plus off-overlap flow | 8, 10 | Attacks the metamers; independent of angle; works on defocused arms | Accuracy on the metamer subset rises above the class prior; flat across angle |
| 4 | Flow-directed constraints plus evidence-weighted persistence | 15, 16 | Converts NP-hard topology to linear-time; upgrades the existing type voting | Accuracy on compound junctions; stable labels across low-flow frames |
| 5 | Blur-aware descriptors as the two-layer gate | 14, feeding 2, 6, 9 | Exploits the defocus already present in the data | Gains where a sharp vessel crosses a defocused one; corrected width and Murray tests across focus |

The ordering trades evidence against payoff. Builds 1 and 2 rest on the strongest published precedent and repair failures that do not depend on noise. If the oracle test shows the plateau lives in graph construction, they could lift single-frame typing substantially on their own.

Builds 3 and 4 are where the identifiability ceiling should break, but they carry tier-C risk. Arms may be shorter than the correlation length of the flux fluctuations. Periodic flow can make lags ambiguous. Flow direction is unreliable in stagnant capillaries.

Build 5 is cheap once module 3 exists, because blur is just another ray attribute.

The second wave holds modules 9, 6, 7, 11, 12, 13 and 17. Module 9 is the most direct neural analogue but is weakest at the shallow angles that matter most. Modules 6 and 7 become much stronger once a reliable two-layer signal exists.

Validation on real video should use labels from annotators who scrub through the video, with more than one annotator per junction. Published junction labels come from single annotators or from grader pairs who agree only about 70–80% of the time, so label noise sets its own ceiling.

## Conclusion

The neural evidence changes what "better junction typing" means. Cortex does not resolve crossings with sharper local detectors. At a crossing it suppresses oriented signals and labels the point with a separate channel. It completes contours only behind a layer it has reason to believe is nearer. It settles continuity with depth, motion and memory gathered over hundreds of milliseconds and many receptive fields. vascnet's two puzzling results, that additive completion hurts and that typing plateaus at zero noise, are what a purely local, ungated reader should produce. For stabilized vessel video, flow is to junctions what disparity is to cortex. It licenses completion, separates transparent layers, distinguishes streaming (crossing) from bouncing (contact), and turns tree reconstruction from an NP-hard problem into a linear-time one.

For absorbing overlaps, depth order is the wrong target. It is commutative in the physics and bistable in perception. Connectivity is the right target, and it becomes identifiable once time is available. The sharpest experiment pits module 9 against module 10. Suppose direction-based transparency detection falls off below an angle set by filter bandwidth while lagged flux correlation stays flat across angle. Then the identifiability diagnosis is confirmed and the fix is in hand. If both fail at shallow angles, the arms are not being measured cleanly, and the single-frame repairs come first. Because no published 2-D method uses flow to decide junction type, a clean result on the factorial and metamer benchmarks would be a contribution in its own right, not only an engineering gain.
