**40 (flux network).** On trees, yes, and version 5's new pieces hold there (verified: a measurement spanning a crossing and a bifurcation; confluences; the grouped set-aside identity). **Has the plan converged?** For sections 35, 36, 37, the moves of 39, and section 40 on trees: yes; what remains is for the harness to measure. ## Tier 1: breaks a section as written

**1. [table: R2 2, R3 1, R3 3, R4 2, R4 12] On a plexus the network does nothing, and the plan claims two things it does.** (a) *Ids.* `join_pieces` (7801–7806) joins every through group and every two-end node into one vessel; through the crossings of a plexus a row is one measurement end to end. The only through-lumens between two measurements are undone joins, which the plan gives no balance. So on a plexus read as crossings every balance pairs arms of one measurement: no sign is constrained (the test's "inert by construction" is the pipeline's case, not only the test's). (b) *Inferred directions.* The plan says "what the network gives a plexus is the inferred directions of unmeasured pieces along whole vessels". An unmeasured vessel on a crossing plexus touches only through-lumens of its own pieces: its flux is in the null space. Measured on the test's 6 × 6 plexus with one row unmeasured: q = 0, σ = 672, direction 0 on all five pieces. The plexus gets nothing. (c) *The anastomotic grid.* "+0 to +4 points" is `grid_case`: per-edge ids and the one-lumen reading. `geometric_reading` (5894–5899) reads a planar four-way as a crossing, and the one-lumen reading of four arms has no test at all (`group_tests` returns {} above three arms, 5815; `overlap` is skipped above three, 5846), so it is never a rival and the margin never asks "crossing or one lumen". With crossing readings on a 12 × 12 harmonic grid and chain ids of 3–6 edges (15% of chains wrong, 70% measured, six seeds), the sign step is active only at chain boundaries (28–47 lumens of 200) and mixed: at 0.45, +4.6, +3.5, +3.3, +2.1, **−1.7**, +3.9 points, vessel flips 2:1, 2:1, 3:2, 2:1, 1:2, 3:1; at 0.15, +1.7, +8.4, +9.4, +7.7, **−8.7** (1:6), +3.9. With the one-lumen reading and the same chain ids: +0, +0, +0, +3.1, +5.2, +0 (flips 1:0, 2:0) at 0.45 and +1.3 to +12.7 (flips 16:2) at 0.15. The table row "with the arms wrongly read as one lumen of four, mixed (3:0 to 1:2 ...)" has it backwards: the fourth review's mixed numbers were the *crossing* reading on an anastomotic grid; the one-lumen reading was the good case. (d) *The margin.* For four arms at an acute angle α the second pairing's turn is α on both groups, so the margin is ≈ (α / 15)² when α ≤ 45° and ∞ above (the rival fails `Z_PASS`): 9 at 45°, 4 at 30°, 1.8 at 20°. A true anastomosis read as a crossing at 60° gets c = 1 − ε and its wrong through-lumens are enforced at cost 13.8. *Change:* withdraw the plexus claims to "a crossing plexus is a no-op; a true anastomosis read as a crossing is a wrong structure the margin cannot see"; score the plexus column as zero expected; and give a four-arm vertex a structural confidence that is P(two vessels, not one lumen), calibrated on the harness from the `overlap` z now and the core z of section 38 later.

**2. [table: R2 2, R3 1, R4 1, R4 4] "1 − ε for a vertex with one structure (three arms; a skeleton join)" is 1 − ε for exactly the structures the new generator cases make wrong.** (a) A *skeleton join* is anything `ridge_crests` closes within `RIDGE_JOIN` = 2 px or thinning fuses: two vessels ending near each other, or a collinear overlap (section 35's own new cases), come out as a two-arm skeleton join. At c = 1 − ε the set-aside costs 13.8 against a flip's 3.5, and the test file asserts the consequence: `test_an_impossible_lumen...` lines 181–182, with the join "not in doubt", `sure.flipped.sum() == 1`, the right daughter of a correct bifurcation turned round, now as the specified behaviour. The two-vessel case in the plan's measured table ("set aside and reported impossible; nothing turned") holds only because the test passes c = 0.8 by hand. (b) A *three-arm vertex* does not have one structure. `set_partitions` (5755, `least = 2`) allows no group of one, so every three-arm vertex is one lumen of three; `geometric_reading`'s own far-side check (5883–5892) reads a thin-stemmed T as a crossing, and that reading is stored as `baseline` and discarded for the partition. A T whose stem dives (truth: a through pair plus a free end) read as one lumen of three is silent: stem 2, 5, 10 against a through vessel of 10 gives −0.24, −0.70, −1.92σ at 0.45 (−0.58, −1.57, −3.33 at 0.15); nothing is set aside, nothing reported until the stem equals the vessel at 0.15. (c) An `extend_ends` fork is a three-arm vertex: the text gives it both 1 − ε ("three arms") and "the extension's confidence for a join it made"; say which. *Change:* a two-arm join's c is P(join right | turn, width, density of the pair), calibrated on the harness as a structural probability (the tests are the only evidence a join has; what the plan rightly refused was a goodness of fit on a vertex that has no rival, not calibration); 1 − ε only for pieces that were one connected ridge before closing. A three-arm vertex whose baseline reads "crossing" gets the through pair as its lumen and c from the far-side evidence. A fork made by an extension carries the extension's confidence whatever its arm count.

**3. [table: R4 14, R1 A5, R2 5, R3 4, R4 5] Two code-level holes in the version-5 pieces.** (a) *The grouped set-aside is skipped at every crossing with a free-only arm.* `fluxnet.py` 265: `if np.linalg.cond(inner) > 1e8: continue`. A crossing whose fourth arm is unmeasured and in no other balance (the field's border, a stub, a vessel that dives) has that row's leverage 1, `I − H` singular for the *group*, and the reading can never be set aside: measured on the test graph with e5 unmeasured and the (e1, e4) lumen 6 against 2 at margin 1.0, `disabled = [False, False]`, cond 8 × 10¹⁰; the same lumen alone is set aside. The grouped identity itself is exact (verified with Huber weights and priors, 1e-8). (b) *The bridge prior has no fallback for an unmeasured free end.* The text says "the arm's measurement, else the lumen's other arm"; at a free end whose arm is unmeasured the lumen's other arms are the bridges themselves. The code (185) takes whatever the caller passes: NaN raises `LinAlgError: SVD did not converge`; 0 is clipped to `floor × 1e-6` and pins the bridge to zero flux (σ 1e-6). *Change:* set aside the rows of a group whose leverage is below 1 (the singular row has zero residual and nothing to gain); define the fallback scale as the end arm's first-solve |q| (as `robust_through` does for unmeasured arms), then the graph's floor, and say so.

## Tier 2: would make a check mislead

**4. [table: R1 A4, R2 1, R3 2, R4 3] Step 6's outputs come from a solve made under readings that step (d) may change.** The order is (b) solve, (c) tests, (d) readings re-taken (the flux test may fail one), (e) frozen, then the closing round "by the rule of `blame`". `blame`, the imbalances, the impossible lumens, the inferred directions and the cycles are all from (b). A reading (d) fails changes its lumens *and* its whole vessels (`join_pieces` follows the partition, 7801), so the pieces of a vessel split at (d) keep one kymograph reading that no longer belongs to one vessel, and the closing round's merge decisions are made on imbalances computed with the failed balance still enforced. "Readings changed by step 6" then counts changes nothing downstream sees. *Change:* one second solve after (d), with no re-reading after it (two solves, not a loop; 4 s at a thousand edges here); `blame`, the handed-on graph and the column come from it; a vessel split at (d) has its pieces as separate measurements with the old direction.

**5. [table: R2 7, R4 9] `blame`'s speed rule never fires.** Binary trees, depth 7 (255 edges), top three levels unmeasured, 70% measured, 10% of measurements × 3, three seeds: on a *pure* tree 88 of 175–184 measured edges sit in three-arm lumens at both ends, yet `blame(...)["speeds"]` is empty at 0.45 and at 0.15 on every seed; the × 3 speeds' studentised residuals have median 0.6–1.2 at 0.45 and 1.3–1.8 at 0.15, with 0 (0.45) or 1–5 (0.15) above 3, against 0–3 right speeds above 3. With edges split into 1–3 pieces joined by through-lumens (the real graph's shape) only 22–33 of 331–354 measured pieces are even eligible, and the rule still fires on none. So a wrong speed is never flagged, at any softness the plan uses, and the studentised residual's false positives are of the order of its true ones at 0.15. That is acceptable only if the plan says it. *Change:* state that section 40 does not detect wrong speeds; make `blame`'s speed output a reported list with the base rate of |studentised| > 3 among right speeds alongside; do not send it to section 39.

**6. [table: R3 7, R4 6] The RMS column is not defined on the pixels it is read on, and self-whitening is 1 for any stationary misfit.** "Whitened by the along-edge autocorrelation measured on the other half of the edges" is a one-dimensional filter along a spline; a core pixel belongs to two or three edges' splines and a halo pixel to none, so on "edges, cores, halos" the whitening is undefined where it matters most. And a whitening by the *residual's* own autocorrelation, held out or not, gives RMS 1 for any misfit that is the same on both halves (a wrong profile shape, a wrong blur): the held-out half protects the filter from fitting the data, not the column from a systematic model error. "1 where the model is right" needs the *noise's* along-line variance, which the quiet pixels give (lines of the same shape drawn through them, or the frames' temporal noise). *Change:* per-edge, the 1-D residual along the spline, whitened by the noise's along-line autocorrelation from quiet lines, RMS by radius; cores scored by the core z; halos by their own residual; the three never pooled.

**7. [table: R1 A1, R2 4, R3 5, R4 7] The core filter's reach, derived for the generator.** Two vessels crossing at 90°, template = blur(sum) − blur(max) of the chord maps, whitened by the mean frame's noise: static texture `SYNTH_TEXTURE` 0.005 at `SYNTH_GRAIN` 2 px (correlation area ≈ 50 px²) plus shot and read noise over the 62 kept frames (√(2500 + 25) / 2500 / √62 = 0.0026, white). z: R 1.5 and 1.5 at blur 0.8, 2.0, 3.5 px → **18.6, 2.4, 0.8**; R 3 and 3 → 44, 17.5, 9.2; R 4 at 2 px → 31; R 5 and 3 at 0.8 and 3.5 → 135. "Decisive for R ≥ 3" holds at every blur the generator makes (0.8–3.5 px); "capillaries marginal" holds only blurred. Three qualifications. The z rides on the white floor (the texture alone gives n_eff < 1 on a 9–16 px overlap), so "n_eff 2–4, c 1.5–2" are in an unstated unit, and on the real burst section 38 must measure the quiet pixels' spectrum, not only its short-lag autocorrelation. The generator has no capillaries, so the order of work's item 4 (the check by radius before section 35) cannot check R ≤ 2. And the cluster modulation left in the 62-frame mean is 12–24% of the chord but correlated over 33–41 px along the vessel (plug flow sweeps 93–217 px): absorbed by the per-window κ, not the core's noise, and the dominant on-vessel residual that finding 6's column would otherwise read as misfit.

**8. [table: R2 17, C5, R4 14] The margin has no floor and a tie costs nothing.** At a tie the margin is 0, c = 0.5, cost 0: the vertex's balances are set aside the moment they have a residual, and the plan says nothing. At the notebook's own `TIE` = 1.0 the cost is 1.0, under a flip's 3.5, which is the regime `fluxnet.py` 155–156 warns against. A finite margin below 3.5 (acute crossings under ≈ 28°) is the only place the margin ever acts, and there it acts as a free pass. *Change:* floor the set-aside cost at one flip's cost, or give a tied vertex no balance and report it as such.

**9. The reach sentence contradicts itself and one of its three categories is empty.** "Bifurcations and confluences with at least two measured arms" against "a bifurcation off an unmeasured trunk gives nothing" in the next sentence: two measured daughters off an unmeasured trunk, one wrong (6 out, 4 in), flips nothing at 0.45 or 0.15 (the trunk takes 2, imbalance NaN). "Joins between two whole vessels" is empty by construction (finding 1a). *Change:* the reach is lumens all of whose arms are measured or pinned by another lumen, which on the real pipeline means bifurcations and confluences only.
