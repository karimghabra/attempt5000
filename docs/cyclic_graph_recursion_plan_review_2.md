# Second adversarial review of `cyclic_graph_recursion_plan.md` and `vascnet/fluxnet.py`

Scope: the plan as it stands after the first review's settlements (A1–A5), the B and C items it left open, the section 40 prototype and its tests, checked against the notebook source (`notebooks/development copy 2.py`, line numbers below are from it unless a file is named) and `vascnet/synth.py`. Each finding says whether it attacks a **settlement** of the first review or is **new**. Numerical claims were run, not estimated; the scripts are not committed.

## Verdict

The settlements mostly moved the problems rather than closing them. A4 (flux as a test) is settled on a mechanism that does not exist: the leave-one-lumen-out imbalance that enters `group_tests` needs one network solve per candidate partition per vertex, not "no second solve", and the prototype's sign step, which the plan presents as "the solve decides", is a greedy one-flip-at-a-time search that on a plexus (the very case section 35 adds) makes the measured directions worse (80% right as read, 74% after the network on a 1104-edge grid) and manufactures directed cycles (298 on a 264-edge grid whose truth has none), which the plan then counts as its third and fourth measures of p. A2's correlation-area division is the wrong statistic for exactly the thin, line-shaped proposals the residual is meant to find (a 1-px line's Δχ² is inflated about 12×, a halo's about 50×; dividing both by 50 throws away three quarters of a thin vessel's evidence); whitening is the method, not the fallback. A1's "nothing was fitted inside a core" is false as the fit is specified (windows every 12 px along every edge, radius knots 36 px apart straddle every core, arms of short edges are read inside the core at line 5398). A5's bridges are rendered without acceptance, which breaks the only argument the plan has for termination and puts unaccepted density into the residual that steps 0 and 5 are judged on. On top of this, `SIGMA_FLUX` means two different things in the notebook (share of the absolute sum, line 5820) and in the prototype (share of the mean, `_lumen_scale`), a factor of two to three in every balance, and the number itself ("measured on the real burst") cannot be checked: the notebook stores no outputs.

What holds: the leave-one-out identities in the prototype are exact (verified against brute-force re-solves), the sign-flip log-odds is the exact change of the penalised χ², the toy tests pass, and on trees the sign step helps (85% → 93% on a 511-edge binary tree). The thinnest path of the first review stands, with one change: section 40 should be run on `whole_graphs` as a *measurement-level* diagnostic (per-measurement studentised residuals, inferred directions scored against `propagate_directions`) before any of its outputs is fed back into a junction reading, and the flip count and post-network cycles should be dropped as measures of p.

## Tier 1: breaks a section as written

**1. (settlement A4) The leave-one-lumen-out test costs a solve per candidate partition per vertex, and tests a lumen with the rest of the same candidate enforced.** Section 27 scores every partition of a vertex's arms (`set_partitions`, line 5755; `choose_partition`, 5921: 4 partitions for four arms, 11 for five, 41 for six, up to `MOST_ARMS` = 6). Each partition's lumens are different balance rows, so the LOO imbalance of a candidate lumen exists only in a solve that contains it; the prototype's own `test_the_imbalance_is_a_test_of_each_lumen_without_its_own_balance` does three solves for three readings. On a 1104-edge graph one solve with flips took 129–151 s (dense `pinv`, line 81); with 300 vertices that is hours per fixation per round, against the plan's "no second solve". Worse, within one candidate the imbalance of lumen A is computed with lumen B of the same candidate enforced (line 154–156 removes one row), so a wrong candidate is tested under its own assumption. And the other vertices' readings, held fixed during the test, were chosen using fluxes inferred through this vertex's current reading: the plan describes a one-shot where there is a coordinate ascent with no stated iteration or stopping rule. *Change:* leave-one-**vertex**-out by a rank-k Woodbury update of the single solve (k = the vertex's current rows), then score every candidate lumen as `z = bᵀq₋ᵥ / sqrt(bᵀC₋ᵥb + s²)` from the same `q₋ᵥ`, `C₋ᵥ`. State the readings → network → readings loop, its order and when it stops.

**2. (new) The sign step is a local search that fails on a plexus, and the plan counts its failures as measurements of p.** Lines 125–139 flip the *first* measured edge (index order) whose LOO odds are negative, re-solve, repeat. Measured: (a) a bifurcation with two of three directions wrong (the trunk and one daughter) makes the network flip the three *right* measurements e2, e4, e7 and reverse the whole tree; the two-flip optimum is 3.5 units cheaper and unreachable one flip at a time (best-odds-first gives the same). (b) On a 12×12 and a 24×24 grid with harmonic flow, one lumen per interior vertex, 70% measured, 15% wrong, σ = 15%: flips 31 (13 right, 18 wrong) and 100 (35 right, 65 wrong); directions right fall from 79.1% to 75.6% and from 80.5% to 74.3%; `directed_cycles` then reports 298 cycles on the 264-edge grid (truth: 0). The cause is that `spread` (line 132) treats every *other* measurement as right with its σ, so a wrong neighbour pushes a right edge over the prior. Section 40 still says the flip share is "a fourth measure of 1 − p" and the cycles "a third way of measuring p": on this grid the flip share reads 13% against a truth of 19% while being 65% wrong, and the cycles are made by the network. B6 of the first review is not answered in the plan text. *Change:* flip per lumen (the complementary set within a lumen is one proposal, which fixes (a)); seed the signs from section 33's whole-vessel directions; add to section 40's check "flips right / wrong" and "directions right after the network not below as read" on a plexus burst; delete the two "measures of p".

**3. (settlement A2) Dividing Δχ² by the correlation area is the wrong correction for line-shaped proposals; whitening is the method, not the fallback.** For a proposal f against noise with normalised autocorrelation ρ, the inflation of its χ² is `∫(f⋆f)(d)ρ(d)dd / (f⋆f)(0)`, which depends on f. With the generator's texture (white noise blurred by `SYNTH_GRAIN` = 2, lines 6376–6377; autocorrelation sigma 2√2), a Gaussian cross-profile of sigma s is inflated across the line by `2sσ_c√π/√(2s²+σ_c²)`: 1.7 for s = 0.5, 3.2 for s = 1, 7.1 for a broad feature; along the line 7.1. A 1-px capillary's Δχ² is inflated about 12×, a halo's about 50×. Dividing both by `AREA` ≈ 40–50 under-counts the capillary's evidence 3–4×, so the step that the residual exists for ("a better place to look for thin vessels", section 38) loses them: a 2σ-per-pixel thin vessel over 50 px fails the price with the division (600/50 = 12 against a price of ~30) and passes it whitened (600/12 = 50). Separately, the residual *along existing edges* is correlated at the knot scale (12–36 px) by the fit's own misfit, so merge, split, fusion and halo proposals, which touch edges, are judged with an `AREA` measured on quiet pixels that is far too small for them. *Change:* whiten each canvas by the measured autocorrelation (one FFT per canvas) and take Δχ² in the whitened domain; measure the autocorrelation on-graph and off-graph separately and report both; drop the "fallback" wording.

**4. (settlement A1) "Nothing was fitted inside a core" is not true of the fit as specified, and the evidence at a core is small after correction.** Section 38 fits "at windows of `KNOT_SPACING` px along every edge" with κ "smoothed with the radius spline's knots" (`RADIUS_KNOTS` × `KNOT_SPACING` = 36 px, lines 6719–6720): an edge under 36 px has one radius and one κ for its whole length including both cores, and every edge's end window lies inside a core. The profile's offset c absorbs a crossing vessel's density near the core. `read_arm` reads an arm *inside* the core when the edge never leaves it ("its outer half has to do", lines 5398–5401), which is the common case at a split crossing, so "arms are read on a ring outside the core" holds only for edges longer than the core. After the area correction the core's evidence for max against sum is about `n_overlap × c² / AREA` with c the per-pixel contrast in noise units: a capillary crossing (overlap ~16 px) at c = 1.5–2 gives 1–2 units, and the composition choice is not priced at all. B3 (blur before composition biases these pixels) is still open. *Change:* exclude windows within the core radius from the fit and from the κ smoothing explicitly; write the core test's evidence and its threshold; render per lumen and blur after composition (B3).

**5. (settlement A5) Bridges rendered without acceptance break termination and the residual.** "Bridges are rendered only above the working cutoff" with a confidence that contains no Δχ²: a bridge over a true termination draws density where there is none, χ² *rises*, and steps 0 and 5 then read a residual with a negative streak nobody accepted. "The objective only falls" is the plan's only termination argument and it no longer covers bridges. The "residual's line mass along the bridge" recorded beside the confidence changes meaning with the cutoff (data − bridge when rendered). The similarity terms do not discriminate where gaps matter most: capillary half-widths 1.5–3 px give log-ratios under 0.7 against `SIGMA_WIDTH` = 0.25 and `SIGMA_DENSITY` = 0.35 (lines 5738–5740), so most capillary pairs pass; the flow terms need a clear speed on both sides, which needs `VELOCITY_LENGTH` = 40 px pieces (line 4971) with flow, exactly what short faint free-ended pieces lack. *Change:* either bridges are never rendered (then the residual line mass under them is a clean field), or rendering is itself the acceptance (Δχ² ≥ 0 required); say which. Report the bridge confidence's `told_apart` separately for thin and thick ends.

**6. (new) `SIGMA_FLUX` means two things, and the number is unverifiable.** `group_tests` divides the net flux by `SIGMA_FLUX × Σ|flows|` (line 5820, the absolute *sum*); `_lumen_scale` (fluxnet.py 57–61) uses `sigma_flux × mean|q̂|` over the lumen's *measured* arms; section 40 says both ("0.45 of the absolute sum" and "the share of the mean absolute flux... `SIGMA_FLUX` is this number"). With 0.45 in both, the prototype's balances are 2× (pairs) to 3× (triples) tighter than the notebook's test and than whatever was measured, so the sign step flips more, Huber trims more, and every LOO imbalance is 2–3× too large, which step 6 reads as persistent imbalances. Because only measured arms set the scale, the same lumen is 2.5× tighter when its small daughter is measured (0.45 × 4) than when its trunk is (0.45 × 10). The comment at line 5743 says the 0.45 is "printed below"; the `.ipynb` holds no outputs (0 of 139 cells), the markdown no number; the first review repeated it as fact. *Change:* define σ_flux on the lumen's fitted mean |q| (iterate once), convert the notebook's number by the arm count or re-measure in the new unit, and print it in section 40's cell.

## Tier 2: would make a check mislead

**7. (new) The robustness claim is vacuous in this regime, and a wrong speed is reported as a junction problem.** With balances at 0.45 × mean and measurement σ at 10–15%, the solution sits on the measurements and all inconsistency goes into the balance residuals; the Huber step on measurement misfits never triggers. Measured: a trunk read as 50 against daughters 6 and 4 keeps weight 1.0, q = 49.9, and the lumen gets an imbalance of −4.4σ (the outlier also inflated its own lumen's scale to 0.45 × 20); the test's own case (trunk 14) has misfits of 0.15σ. Step 6's "inconsistent fluxes lower the confidence of the measurements" has no mechanism. *Change:* output the per-measurement studentised residual `(q̂ − without)/spread` (the leverage identity already gives it in the sign loop) as the wrong-speed detector; robustify the balance residuals, not the measurement misfits.

**8. (new) `directed_cycles` enumerates every simple directed path and counts directions of no confidence.** The DFS (fluxnet.py 195–205) visits every path from each start through higher vertices, so it is exponential on a DAG plexus with zero cycles (9×9 grid, 144 edges: 0.09 s, ×4 per step; the 1104-edge grid's enumeration had not finished after 15 CPU-minutes). `direction` (line 46–48) counts every edge with finite sigma: an all-free daughter pair gets sigma ≈ 10⁵ and still a direction, so cycles are reported through edges whose direction is noise. The docstring's "fine for graphs of hundreds of edges" is untested. C2 is open. *Change:* strongly connected components over edges with `|q|/σ` above a cutoff; report one cycle per component.

**9. (new) The renderer's blur is in the wrong domain and `_branch_od` is misdescribed.** `_branch_od` (synth.py 263–315) returns the *unblurred* chord OD; `render` (448–453) takes max within a tree, converts to transmittance, blurs the transmittance, multiplies across trees. The notebook's generator blurs the *density* per tree (lines 6414–6417). The plan renders blurred OD per edge. By Jensen, −log(mean e^{−OD}) ≤ mean OD, with the gap ≈ var(OD)/2 across the kernel: for OD 0.3–0.5 (the real burst's range, `DENSITY_RANGE` to 0.5, line 1621) a negative streak of 0.01–0.03 at every thick vessel's flank, several noise units at the generator's 0.005, which step 0 fits as a negative halo and R_od absorbs. The harness cannot show it because its generator blurs OD too. *Change:* render transmittance per lumen canvas, blur, take −log; make section 35's generator do the same, or state that the harness is blind to this.

**10. (settlement A3) Merge and split are judged where the evidence is not, and the merge already exists.** The rendering difference between two T's and one crossing is confined to the core's few pixels; the decision's consequences are topological (`join_pieces`, the lumens, the whole-vessel speed). Δχ² on the core is nearly blind to it, so the priced rule will neither accept nor refuse merges on their merits. `fuse_split_crossings` (line 5457, `FUSE_FACTOR` = 2.5 widths / sin, `FUSE_STRAIGHT`) already runs inside `vessel_graph` (5546) on section 29's graphs; the plan calls the two-T's crossing "the skeleton's commonest failure" without saying what the new merge adds. The split rule cites `where_axes_meet`, which returns one least-squares point clipped to the core (5903–5919) and cannot say whether two pairs of axes meet at two places. "No state is final until the last round" is also wrong as a guarantee: the damped centerline shift of step 3 and the bridges change the residual outside the priced objective, so a removed edge can be re-drawn on a residual that moved for reasons no proposal paid for. *Change:* merge and split by junction-cell rate and the partition reading, with Δχ² as a veto only; cite `fuse_split_crossings` and state the difference; write the global objective (Σχ²_whitened + Σ_edges params × ln(N_e / AREA)) and list what moves outside it.

**11. (new) `PARAMETER_COST` is defined as computed and sold as swept.** Section 39: "`PARAMETER_COST` = ln(number of valid pixels the proposal touches / `AREA`)... it is the one swept setting"; section 42 sweeps it on the real burst. Either a multiplier is swept or the definition is wrong. The sweep's objective (repeatability, loose ends) rewards systematic additions (B7, unanswered). *Change:* name the swept multiplier; add the share of directed cycles and impossible junctions (section 33's `impossible`) to the sweep.

**12. (new) Section 37's "already exists" claims.** `repeated` (4235) returns one scalar per fixation from `found_again` (2206), not a share per edge; a per-edge version is new code. `static_scores` saved for section 29 is `orientation_scores` (7178–7181), the score *before* the interactions, which answers B5 (plaid cells would read the un-tuned score, where the weaker line survives) but contradicts section 38's "the flank inhibition suppresses a thin vessel at a thick one's flank", which happens only in `static_fields`, which section 29 does not use. `tube_reach` and `profile_across` do what the plan says. "The ring starts outside the core" holds only for edges longer than the core (finding 4).

**13. (new) Section 37's check cannot pass on junctions, and the notebook already said so.** The consolidation adds connectivity (closing within `RIDGE_JOIN` = 2 px, `extend_ends` into a line within `RIDGE_REACH` = 16 px, lines 6806–6823); a confidence cutoff on the plain ridges removes but never joins, so junction recall of the cyclic graph is bounded by the plain ridges' forks at every cutoff. Section 31's result (7682): keeping pieces gave twice the loose ends and no more junctions on the held-out bursts. The check scores centerline precision/recall only; it must score junction recall, and B8's number (the `told_apart` below which the principle is dropped) is still absent.

**14. (new) The tests assert weaker claims than the text.** `test_a_wrong_direction_is_turned_round_by_the_network` flips the one edge contradicted by 12 units at both of its ends; no test has a wrong direction on a leaf (contradicted at one end) or a wrong majority at a junction (finding 2a). `test_balances_are_soft` is parametrised over `sigma_flux` but asserts the same inequality for each value; it never checks that softer balances move the fluxes less. `test_an_enforced_balance_would_hide_its_own_imbalance` asserts `|fitted| < |imbalance|`, which is the identity `imbalance = fitted / sqrt(1 − h)`, not a behaviour. `test_the_imbalance_is_a_test...` passes `> 2.0` at 2.07. `chi2` (157–158) sums `weight × r²`, which under Huber is `c|r|`, not the Huber loss `2c|r| − c²`, so `compare_readings` compares a quantity that is not a likelihood whenever a weight is below 1; the balance scales also differ between readings (lines 116–117), so the log-determinant terms a likelihood ratio would carry are dropped. *Change:* add the leaf and majority-wrong cases, a monotonicity assertion over `sigma_flux`, and a plexus case scored on directions right.

## Verified and held

- The measurement LOO identities (fluxnet.py 131–132): prediction `(q − h q̂)/(1 − h)` and variance `C_ee/(1 − h)` match brute-force re-solves to 1e-12.
- The balance-row identity (154–156): `(Bq)_r / (s √(1 − h_r))` equals the net flux predicted without the row over `√(s² + bᵀC₋ᵣb)` exactly.
- The sign log-odds (135) is exactly the change of the minimised χ² plus the flip cost `2·ln(p/(1 − p))`, so each flip strictly lowers the penalised χ² and the loop terminates (the cap `count + 1` can cut it short after un-flips).
- `lumens_of` and `velocity_out`'s sign convention agree with `read_arm` (line 5407: +1 where the edge's points leave the node).
- `choose_partition` takes the most groups among partitions with no test beyond `Z_PASS`; groups of four or more carry no tests (5807–5809) and "fit" by default; with `evidence="tests"` flow changes a reading only when the geometric one fails a flow test.
- The notebook's `flux` test cannot fail a partition: `|Σ|/Σ|·| ≤ 1` gives at most 2.2σ against `Z_PASS` = 3; it only moves the score. The network's z can exceed it, which is a behaviour change the plan should state.
- `_branch_od`'s profile, max within a tree and multiplied transmittance across trees are as the plan says; the blur is not (finding 9); `reflex` exists and the plan ignores it.
- The generator conserves volumetric flux at a split (speed ∝ share^{1/3}, radius ∝ share^{1/3}, M·v ∝ share), composes max within a tree and sum across trees, and its texture is correlated over ≈50 px as the first review computed.
- `tube_reach` (6831), `profile_across` (3721), `contract_cores` (5335), `join_pieces` (7773), `where_axes_meet` (5903), `direction_counts`/`reliability` (8091–8131) exist and do what the plan cites, with the caveats in findings 10 and 12.
- The 12 prototype tests pass (0.37 s). On binary trees with bifurcation lumens, 70% measured and 15% wrong, the sign step improves directions (84.1% → 89.8% at 127 edges; 85.1% → 93.2% at 511 edges) with 2 wrong flips in 32.
