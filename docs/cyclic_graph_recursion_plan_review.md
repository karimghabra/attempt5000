# Adversarial evaluation of `cyclic_graph_recursion_plan.md`

The plan is attacked section by section: what it claims, where the claim breaks on the notebook's own evidence or on the physics, and what would have to change. Findings are ranked: **A** breaks a section as written, **B** weakens it enough that its check would mislead, **C** is a cost or an omission. The last part says what survives and what the thinnest path through it is.

## A. Breaks the section as written

**A1. The residual along an edge is nearly empty by construction (section 38).** The per-edge profile fit takes R, κ and σ from the data across the edge, so the rendering of an edge reproduces that data along its own centerline; the "residual profile per vessel" then contains only what the smoothing between knots (36 px) left out. The interpretation table's first two rows ("positive / negative along the whole edge") cannot occur, and the proposal's central picture, a residual profile per vessel that says "good only at the ends", holds only because the ends are cores, where the parameters were not fitted. The residual carries information in exactly three places: the cores, the overlaps between edges, and off the graph. The section should say so, and the classifier should be restricted to features shorter than the knot spacing (a crossing vessel's bump, about 2R of the crossing vessel) and to cores. Alternatively fit κ once per whole vessel and R once per knot, so that a local departure is left in the residual by design; that is a choice to make, not an accident to discover.

*Settled.* The residual is the whole network's and is read at cores, off the graph, and along edges only for what the fit cannot absorb; section 38 now says so and its table is restricted accordingly. A new vessel is proposed only on evidence outside the pixels of existing edges (the residual's ridges off the graph, the tails of a shallow crossing linked by the association field). κ stays free per window; the one case this misses, a vessel lying exactly along an existing edge with nothing emerging, is accepted as a stated limit of the still and counted on the harness, with the kymograph's two families of streaks left as a later test that does not add a vessel on its own.

**A2. The chi-square price assumes independent pixels; the residual's noise is not (section 39).** The accept rule charges ln(N) per parameter over N touched pixels (BIC). The mean density's noise is correlated over the tissue texture's grain (about 2 px, `SYNTH_GRAIN`; on the real burst the quiet pixels show it) and over the registration's resampling. A Gaussian correlation of 2 px makes the effective number of independent pixels smaller by about 4πσ² ≈ 50, so every Δχ² is inflated by that factor against a price that is not. The recursion will then accept bridges and stubs that explain nothing but correlated noise, and will accept them more the larger they are. The fix is cheap and has to come before any round is run: measure the residual's autocorrelation off the graph on the quiet pixels, and divide χ² by the correlation area (or whiten the residual). Section 39's "one setting" is then two, and the second is measured, not swept.

*Settled.* The price is corrected by the residual's correlation area, measured once per fixation on the quiet pixels off the graph (`residual_area`), with whitening as the fallback for an anisotropic correlation; section 39's accept rule now carries it. It is measured, not swept.

**A3. There is no removal, no vertex merge and no vertex split among the proposals (section 39).** The objective only falls, which is sold as the reason the recursion ends; it is also the reason a wrong acceptance in round 1 lives forever. A bridge accepted before the halo under it was fitted, a composition chosen before a missing arm was found, two T's that are one crossing (the common skeleton failure the junction cells are supposed to catch) with no proposal that merges the two vertices: none of these can be undone. Add three proposal kinds: remove an edge (its Δχ² is already computed as the seventh confidence term), merge two vertices within a core into one, split a vertex into two. With removal the objective still only falls, so termination is kept.

*Settled.* Removal, merge and split are proposals of the recursion (section 39, step 3b), accepted by the same priced Δχ²; a removed edge stays in the graph undrawn and can be drawn again; the two-T's crossing is merged on the junction cell's `n` = 4 against degrees of 3. The objective still only falls.

**A4. The flux evidence between readings is biased towards the less specific reading (section 40, and the prototype's `compare_readings`).** A reading with fewer balances fits at least as well as one with more: a single lumen of four arms (one equation) always beats a crossing (two equations), and the prototype's own test asserts this ("one lumen fits anything that balances"). Section 27 avoids the same trap by choosing the most specific hypothesis that fits. The flux's likelihood ratio must therefore only be taken between readings with the same number of balances, or carry a specificity term (the volume of flux patterns each reading allows, which `direction_likelihood` already counts as `2^n − 2` for the directions). As written, "a crossing whose two through-vessels each balance, against a bifurcation that does not" is told apart, but "crossing against one lumen" is always lost.

*Settled, and in the prototype.* The flux network does not compare readings. Each lumen's imbalance is measured with the network solved without that lumen's own balance (leave-one-lumen-out) and enters section 27's `group_tests` as the `flux` test with the network's sigma; section 27's most-specific-reading-that-fits rule handles specificity as it does for every other test. `compare_readings` becomes a diagnostic restricted to readings with equal numbers of balances. The alternatives were a specificity term (one more thing to calibrate) or restricting the ratio to equal balance counts (silent between a crossing and one lumen, which is where the question most often is).

**A5. The bridging test rejects exactly the gaps it is meant to close (section 39, step 1).** A gap exists because the vessel faded: it dove through the focal plane or lost contrast. Its density along the bridge line *is* a dip. The rule "no dip deeper than `SIGMA_DENSITY` below the two ends" refuses it, and keeps only bridges over places that are not gaps. The dip's depth distinguishes two cases, a vessel fading (dip to a share of the ends' density, smooth) and two vessels ending (dip to background, within one blur of each end), and the test should be on the dip's shape, not its depth. And the reach, 3 widths with a floor of `RIDGE_REACH` = 16 px, is not "large gaps" in any sense the proposal meant: a vessel that passes under a thick one or out of focus for 40–100 px is out of reach. Large gaps need a path search along the weak score and the association field (section 21's support, section 22's bridging already do a short version), with flow as the decisive evidence: flux lost at one end, flux appearing at the other, same direction, similar size. The plan has that evidence ranked second; for large gaps it is the only one.

*Settled.* A bridge is never accepted or rejected. It is a proposed continuation with a confidence from how alike the vessel is on the two sides of the gap (radius, line mass, blur, bend, direction, speed, pulse phase), read outside the cores; nothing in the gap goes into it. The reach is ten widths, the path the smoothest curve between the ends' directions, rivals are left to the flux network with the bridges' confidences as prior weights, and the residual's line mass under the bridge and the kymograph's continuity across it are recorded beside the confidence, not folded in.

## B. Weakens the section; its check would mislead

**B1. The harness cannot see the flux network's main systematic (sections 35, 40).** The synthetic bursts move clusters along a segment at one speed (`segment["speed"]`): plug flow. The kymograph on a real vessel reads the cells' pattern speed near the centerline, not the mean speed over the cross-section; the ratio between the two depends on the vessel's size (about 0.5 for Poiseuille, higher for red cells in capillaries). So M × v is conserved at a real bifurcation only up to a size-dependent factor, and the harness, where the factor is 1 everywhere, will report balances that the real burst does not have. `SIGMA_FLUX` = 0.45 was measured on the real burst's bifurcations and absorbs this, which is why it is so wide, and why the prototype found the flux evidence weak. Either the generator gets a velocity profile across the vessel (clusters at several radial positions, the outer ones slower), or the plan states that the flux balance on the real burst is calibrated only by `SIGMA_FLUX` and that the harness's flux numbers are an upper bound.

**B2. The two radii are not separable for thin vessels (section 38).** For a vessel whose radius is under the picture's blur (section 31 measures the blur per fixation; thin capillaries are 1.5–2 px in half-width against a blur of the same order), the chord profile convolved with the blur is a Gaussian whatever R is: R_od and σ trade off freely, and the "two radii disagree → overlap, halo or twin" classifier fires on every thin vessel for no reason. Fix σ per fixation to the measured picture blur and fit only R and κ; use R_od only where R > σ.

**B3. Blur before composition is wrong at the cores, which is where the composition is judged (section 38).** Each edge is blurred in its own canvas, then composed by max within a lumen. The max of two blurred profiles is not the blur of their union; the difference is concentrated at the core of a bifurcation, exactly where step 4 reads the residual's sign to decide between one lumen and two. The fusion test is then biased by the renderer's own approximation. Compose the unblurred lumen first (vascnet renders per tree for this reason), then blur; with per-edge canvases this means per-lumen canvases at the cores.

**B4. The halo competes with the background fit (sections 38, 39).** Section 12's background is a local mean of `BACKGROUND_SIGMA` = 8 px over quiet pixels, and section 16 removes a 64 px smear. Any halo wider than about 8 px has already been partly absorbed into the background, and the fit's overshoot around thick vessels leaves *negative* halos. A halo model that is positive only, with σ_h unbounded, fits the wrong thing. Allow negative h, bound σ_h below `BACKGROUND_SIGMA`, and check first on the real burst whether the thick vessels' surroundings are above or below zero.

**B5. The plaid cells may have nothing to read (section 36).** The tuning over orientation (`tune_orientation`) sharpens each hypercolumn to one orientation; it holds back the surround between orientations far apart (`protect_crossings`) so that a crossing's weaker line survives, but how much survives is not measured anywhere. Before plaid cells are built, count on a synthetic crossing how much of the weaker line's score remains after the interactions at the core, and decide whether plaid cells read the field before or after them (`orientation_scores` or `static_fields`; section 29 saves one set of 16 layers as `static_scores`, and the plan does not say which it is).

**B6. Counting flips as a measure of 1 − p is circular (section 40).** The network turns a direction round where the rest of the network disagrees with it; counting those as "wrong directions" assumes the network is right. Under a wrong partition the network flips right measurements. The three-in junctions and the sides are measures because they need no model of the junction; the flips are not. Report the flips, do not add them to p.

**B7. Repeatability across fixations is blind to shared mistakes (section 42).** `PARAMETER_COST` is to be swept on the real burst by repeatability and loose ends, as section 31 swept its setting. A bridge over a shadow, a stub along a thick vessel's flank, a halo read as a vessel, are all repeated in every fixation, because the fixations see the same vessels and the same shadows. The sweep will reward anything systematic. Section 31 at least swept a rule that only removed; here the setting adds. Sweep it on repeatability *and* on the share of directed cycles and impossible junctions (section 33's count), which a systematic addition raises.

**B8. "Nothing is deleted" moves the deletion, it does not remove it (section 37).** The notebook's own result (section 31) is that keeping more pieces gave twice the loose ends at lower repeatability and no more junctions on the held-out bursts. The principle survives only if the confidence separates true from false pieces well enough that a cutoff beats consolidation by length. That is a falsifiable claim with a number (`told_apart` of edge confidence between true and false pieces on the harness), and the plan should state the number at which the principle is dropped and section 29's consolidation kept, rather than leaving the cutoff to "the consumer that comes next".

**B9. The junction cell's one ring per size does not handle a thin arm at a thick vessel (section 36).** The ring that matches the thick vessel (its half-width plus `RING_MARGIN`) is far from a thin daughter's core; on that ring the thin arm is one or two pixels wide against the thick vessel's blurred flank, and the scale competition at those pixels can still be won by the thick vessel's width. The plan dismisses a kernel per arm width without a check. vascnet's test `test_thin_vessel_under_thick_vessel_is_crossover` is the nearest existing case; add a thin-daughter-of-a-thick-trunk case to section 36's check before deciding.

## C. Costs and omissions

**C1. The hypercolumns on the residual every round (step 5) is section 20's full cost per round per fixation**, the heaviest step in the notebook. Restrict it to windows around residual bumps above `T_HIGH`, or run it once after the last round.

**C2. The sign step and the solve scale badly.** One flip at a time with a dense solve per flip is O(E) solves of O(E³); the real burst's graph has hundreds to thousands of pieces. Use a sparse solver and update all flips whose odds are beyond a margin in one pass; the leave-one-out identity needs only the covariance's diagonal, which a sparse factorisation gives. `directed_cycles` enumerates elementary cycles, exponential in a plexus; replace by strongly connected components (a component with more than one vertex holds a directed cycle) and report one cycle per component.

**C3. The flow field is not used.** The proposal lists oriented flow cells among the layers; the plan uses only `flow_share`. Section 26's `field_agreement` and `combined_field` exist; the flow field's ridges are a second source of edges for vessels the static field underrates (fast, faint ones), and its orientation's agreement with the static one is a confidence term that costs nothing.

**C4. The big vessels' directions will come from inference only.** On the real burst the fastest vessels are above the trackable limit (section 32 flags them), so the largest fluxes, the ones a balance would learn most from, are unmeasured, and their direction is inferred from the small arms. The digraph's most visible edges are its least measured; say so in the picture (the plan's `measured` flag exists; make the drawing use it).

**C5. The confidences are called log-likelihood ratios and summed before anything calibrates them.** The six terms of section 37 are heuristics in noise units and field lengths; summing them assumes independence (strength and repeatability are not independent) and a null that is never stated. Section 41 calibrates at the end; the bridging rivalry and step 3 use the confidences before that. Calibrate the bottom-up terms on section 37's graph alone, on the harness, before section 39 is written.

**C6. Sections 36–40 all have to work before any of them is scored end to end.** The plan's order is its section numbers. The cheap, low-risk parts are the graph with confidences (37, mostly existing code) and the flux network on the whole-vessel graphs that already exist (`whole_graphs` carry arms with flux and readings); both give numbers on the real burst without a renderer. The renderer and the recursion (38, 39) are the expensive and risky parts, and A1–A3 are all in them.

**C7. The ablation table grows by about six columns and five rows.** With 16 held-out bursts and 95% intervals, something among sixty new cells will look like an effect by chance. Name in advance the one column each section has to move (section 36: junction recall at equal precision; 37: `told_apart` of edge confidence; 38: residual RMS off the graph; 39: found-and-typed-right; 40: directions right among inferred), and treat the rest as description.

## What survives

- The junction cell as a ring reading of the existing score (36), with B5 and B9 as its first two checks.
- The graph that keeps everything with a confidence (37), as a claim with a number attached (B8), not as a principle.
- The renderer as the per-pixel form of section 27's overlap test, and the residual at cores, overlaps and off the graph (38, after A1, B2, B3, B4).
- The recursion with a price per parameter (39), after A2 and A3; its order (halos first) stands.
- The flux network (40) as the way to infer directions with weights and to find the inconsistent measurement, with A4 and B6 fixed; its evidence between readings is weaker than the plan says and the harness overstates it (B1).

## The thinnest path

1. Section 37 on the real burst and the harness: the graph from the plain ridges with the six confidence terms, and the one number of B8. Half of the code exists.
2. Section 40 on `whole_graphs` as they are: balances from the geometric readings, fluxes from the whole-vessel speeds; report inferred directions, imbalances, directed cycles and flips on the real burst, and score inferred directions on the harness against `propagate_directions`. No renderer needed.
3. Section 36's two checks (B5, B9) on synthetic crossings and thin daughters before any cell map is built for the whole frame.
4. Only then the renderer (38) with A1, B2, B3, B4 settled, and the recursion (39) with A2, A3 and A5 settled, each on one synthetic burst before the harness.
