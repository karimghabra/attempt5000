## Verdict on handoff readiness

**Could an implementer build it from the text alone?** Not sections 37 and 38(a) as written, for four reasons that are each a sentence or two to fix but that currently point the implementer at the wrong map, a stage that does not exist, a price with no unit, and a gate with no value. Section 40 and the watching are buildable (they exist). Section 36, the moves of 39 and section 42 are as the fifth review left them.

**Would what they build be sound?** The renderer, the self-consistency number and the per-channel residual are sound *once the ridge map is the one section 29 actually uses*: with the pre-interaction winner, the exact truth rendered noiselessly reaches a self-consistency of 0.996–0.999 in both directions on two synthetic bursts, and the data's own repeatability is 0.995–1.0; with the post-interaction field the plan names, the same comparison reaches 0.28–0.42 and 59–73% of the data's ridge pixels are noise. The central rationale of 38(a), "a thin vessel at a thick one's flank is still visible per channel", is false at the flank and true only two to four pixels off it (measured).

**Must change before handoff:** findings 1–4. **Can wait for the harness:** everything in tiers 2 and 3, which are calibrations, undefined corners and cost figures that step 4 of Part III will measure anyway, provided the text says so.

## Tier 1: breaks a section as written

**1. Section 37's ridges are taken from the wrong field, and the "what exists" table says the wrong thing about it.** The table row for `static_strength`, `static_orientation`, `half_width` says "the score after the interactions", and the initialisation's step 1 says "`ridge_crests` on the static strength after the interactions". The notebook does the opposite: section 29 saves `static_strength` as `winning_neurons(orientation_scores[number], radius_maps[number])` (7171), the winner of section 20's score *before* section 21; `ridge_front` (7088–7101), which every harness row goes through, does the same (7094). Section 21's field (`static_fields`, 3743) is used by section 22's chains, not by section 29.

The two maps are not alike. On synthetic burst seed 1 at the generator's noise, `ridge_crests` on the pre-interaction winner gives 2,335 pixels of which 6% lie more than `REPEAT_WITHIN` = 3 px from any true line; on the post-interaction field it gives 7,872 pixels of which 73% do, in 471 off-truth components (the facilitation raises chance rows of noise, as the docstring of `crest_neurons` warns, 3659–3668, and the hysteresis at `T_HIGH` = 4.5 keeps them). After the plan's own skeleton steps (closing, `SPUR_LENGTH` pruning, `extend_ends`): 1,852 px with 2% noise against 4,406 px with 59% noise in 137 off-truth pieces; seed 2: 4% against 69%. The consequences run through 37–39: the self-consistency number with the *exact truth* rendered is 0.996–0.999 in both directions on the pre-interaction map and 0.999 / 0.28–0.42 on the post-interaction one, where "data in render" is bounded by the noise share and can never reach the 1 the plan calls a fixed point; step 5 of the recursion would receive 137 noise pieces per 384 × 512 frame (about 700 on 1000 × 1000) as "positive ridges off the splines", each a candidate move; and the edge confidence's `told_apart` would be computed over a population that is mostly noise.

*Change:* say "the pre-interaction winner, as `ridge_front` and section 29's saved maps have it" in the table row, in step 1 of the initialisation, and in `score_render`'s row ("its ridges after the full pipeline" must mean competition then `ridge_crests`, without section 21). If the author means the post-interaction field, section 29's pruning (`LINE_LEAST`, `RIDGE_NETWORK`) comes back and the first principle is gone; the plan has to choose and the choice is the pre-interaction map.

**2. The "stage before the scale competition, 16 × 8" does not exist, and the reason given for using it is wrong where it matters.** (a) `orientation_scores[n]` is `(16, rows, columns)` (3193, 3287): the widths compete inside `one_orientation` (3323–3332) and only the winner is kept; the "what exists" row "16 orientations × 8 widths" is false, and section 36's "16 layers" is right. A 16 × 8 stack is new code (25 lines, done for this review) and costs 512 MB per stack in float32 on 1000 × 1000, so a gigabyte for data plus render per fixation; the plan must say so and choose float16 or a per-window computation. The noise constants transfer (eight numbers, 3316: the robust sd of the *even* field on the quiet sample); the floor does not: it is defined per *winner* width on quiet pixels (3340–3345) and has no per-channel meaning, so `score_render`'s "the same constants" is underdetermined for a channel that never wins. Running `orientation_hypercolumns` on the render as it stands divides by a noise of zero.

(b) The flank claim. Thick vessel R = 6 and thin R = 1.5 running parallel, the thick rendered exactly, generator noise, per-channel residual in noise units at the thin vessel's line: at a clear gap of 0 or 1 px the six channels of widths 1.5–8.5 are **exactly 0 in the data itself**, because the thick vessel's edge drives `|odd|` above the even answer and `complex_unit` clips (3274); the only residual is in the width-12 and 17 channels, +2.3 to +4.9, with −3.4 to −5.2 at the empty mirror flank. That is an asymmetry of the thick vessel, not a thin line: a thick spline 0.5 px off reproduces it with the opposite sign (−1.8, −2.7 at the thin line; +0.4, +3.4 at the mirror) and R 10% too large cancels it (+0.6, −3.2). The thin vessel vanishes in the competition, as the plan says, but it had vanished before it. At a clear gap of 2–3 px the width-1.5 channel wakes (1.9, then 7.2) while the competition still picks width 17 (17.6, 18.2): the one regime where the per-channel comparison sees what the winner hides. From 5 px on the thin channels carry 8–10 and the thick channels are 0 at the thin line, so the winner map shows the thin vessel anyway. A thin vessel crossing the thick at 90 degrees is fine: its channel is 0 within 8 px of the thick centre and 16.7 at 12 px, so "tails on both sides" holds.

*Change:* restate the rationale as "a thin vessel running beside a thick one with two to four pixels of clear gap", drop "at its flank", and name the flank case as a limit beside the collinear overlap of section 38 (a vessel touching a thick one's wall leaves a width-12/17 asymmetry that a position error of the thick spline also leaves). State the 16 × 8 stack as new code with its memory, and define the per-channel normalisation as noise[width] only, no floor, or a floor measured per channel on the data's quiet pixels.

**3. `refine_knots(chain, price)` cannot be computed: its residual and its price are in different units, and the price depends on a λ that exists only after step 7.** The knot refinement's residual is `spline_vessel`'s rms distance in pixels (7028); "section 39's price" is λ ln(n) per parameter in the units of χ²_topo, a sum of squared per-channel differences in noise units. Nothing in the text converts one to the other. And λ is "chosen on the real burst as the largest value at which the shares of impossible junctions and of directed cycles do not exceed the consolidated graph's": impossible junctions and directed cycles come from the flux network's second solve (step 6, in the last round), so λ is known after the recursion has run, while `refine_knots` is one of the "first five units" whose gate is acceptance step 1. The implementer has a circular dependency at the very first unit.

*Change:* price the knots in the ridge's own unit with no λ: accept a halving when the fall of the summed squared distance divided by `RIDGE_PRECISION`² exceeds ln(n) per added coefficient (a BIC with σ = `RIDGE_PRECISION`); then the initialisation needs nothing from section 39, and λ prices structural moves only. Measured on seed 1's 32 pieces, the staircase residual is 0.28 px median, 0.44 max at 12 px knots, so at `RIDGE_PRECISION` = 0.5 the refinement fires on nothing on the generator's smooth vessels and the rule is only exercised on the real burst.

**4. Acceptance step 1's gate counts a term the harness cannot produce.** The gate is `told_apart` ≥ 0.75 "on the five bottom-up terms", one of which is repeatability per edge, `found_again` against *another fixation's* ridges. `synthetic_burst` (6347–6432) makes one mean frame; `harness_scores` (7591–7600) reads one burst per seed; there is no second fixation and no map between fixations in the harness. The term has no value on the held-out bursts, so the number the plan says decides whether the rest is built cannot be computed as written.

*Change:* the gate on four terms on the harness, repeatability reported on the real burst only; or give the generator a second fixation (the same truth with fresh noise, as this review did to get the data's own repeatability of 0.995–1.0; a shift between the two would make `CHANCE_SHIFT`'s chance level meaningful).

## Tier 2: would make a check mislead or an implementer build the wrong thing

**5. The topology residual's zero point on a correctly drawn spline is about −1 noise unit, and the per-channel difference is censored, not Gaussian.** A thin line R = 1.5 at κ = 0.025 rendered exactly: data − render on the centre in the render's best channel is −0.78 ± 0.22; at κ = 0.012 (score 8.5) −1.16 ± 0.24; R = 3 (score 46) −0.91 ± 0.18; every width's channel at the line's orientation lies between −0.3 and −2.0. The cause is `complex_unit`'s rectification: the data's `|odd|` and `|halves|` carry noise with a positive expectation and the render's carry none. So a "negative ridge on a spline" of one noise unit is the signature of a *right* spline, and step 3b's reading needs a threshold above it; on the synthetic burst the bias is diluted to −0.2 by the cluster modulation, so a threshold set there would be wrong on a clean capillary. Off the vessels 80–89% of channel-pixels are exactly 0 (white noise through the bank), so χ²_topo is not χ²-distributed and only its differences under λ mean anything. The footprint count is roughly right: the rectified channel's correlation area (sum of ρ² over lags) is 7.8, 12, 24, 44, 127, 227, 403, 726 px² for the eight widths against the plan's 2.5 w² = 5.6, 11, 22.5, 44, 90, 181, 360, 722, while the even field's is nine times larger. *Change:* measure the zero point per channel on the initial model's own splines at round 0 and subtract it; say the footprint count applies to the rectified channel.

**6. Done condition 3 names no mechanism.** "A disagreement inside a tube is a measurement problem (a width, a κ, the blur, a spline placed off the crest) and is fixed here": of the four, only κ has a loop (step 4's Gauss–Seidel); the half-width is `spline_vessel`'s `width` spline of the hypercolumn's half-width, the blur is `picture_blur` (6824, one quantile per fixation), and the place is the knot-refined fit to the ridge, none of them re-fitted against the render. The condition can fail with nothing to do. *Change:* either make it a report (the within-tube agreement at round 0, which on the pre-interaction map was 0.991–0.998 against the data's own 0.995–1.0, so it will usually pass) or give it the one mechanism that exists, the centerline shift of step 3, run inside the tubes before the rounds.

**7. The order of the acceptance steps contradicts itself.** Step 5 (the core z table) says "after section 35 has capillaries"; section 35 is step 6. Step 4's gate "within the tubes the agreement reaches the data's own repeatability" has the same missing value on the harness as finding 4. *Change:* generator first (it is also what finding 4 needs), then the z table; the tube gate on the real burst.

**8. The sub-pixel initialisation needs a rule for the pixels that have no crest neuron, and for those with three.** `crest_neurons` (3659–3692) reads the post-interaction field and thins in orientation as well as position; on seed 1 and 2, 79–90% of the plan's skeleton pixels have a crest neuron at the pixel, 97–99% within one pixel, and a pixel can hold up to three (different k). The offset is across the neuron's own orientation, which differs from the pixel's winning pre-interaction orientation at bends. *Change:* take the neuron of the pixel's winning orientation (or the nearest in k), fall back to the integer pixel, and say so in `ridge_lines`' row.

**9. The recorder's state for the rounds is not "a few thousand numbers", and the recorder does not copy.** A 1000 × 1000 render is 4 MB in float32 and the residual's ridges 1 MB as a map; at `every` = 5 plus every taken move, a round of a thousand candidates keeps 200–400 states, 1–2 GB. `Recorder.__call__` stores the dict it is given (watch.py 37: `dict(state, candidate=...)`, a shallow copy), so a fit that updates its render in place records one array many times; `fluxnet.py`'s `report` (255–262) copies every array explicitly, and the plan's new drawing functions must too. *Change:* record the changed lumen canvases and the window's residual, not the frame; keep the render only on taken states; say that the fit copies.

## Tier 3: cost or omission

**10. The windowed re-score equals the full one only under two conditions the plan does not state.** Widths 6 and up are computed on grids reduced by 2, 2, 4 and 5 (`reduction`, 3217) with `cv2.resize`, so a window whose origin or size is not a multiple of 20 px never agrees with the full frame in the width-12 channel (0.277 noise units at every margin tried, 16–160 px). Aligned to 20 px, agreement within 0.005 in every channel needs a margin of 110 px (at 70 px the width-17 channel is off by 0.075, at 30 px by 0.5), not "one footprint" (the widest σ along is 42.5 px). Cost on this machine (4 cores): a full `orientation_hypercolumns` on 1000 × 1000 is 3.7 s with four threads (11.3 s with one), a 320 px window 2.0 s; the window saves a factor of two to five, and a round of a thousand candidates is half an hour to an hour of scoring. The ridges need no interactions (finding 1), which removes the 6.6 s `lateral_interaction` would add per evaluation. The frame's own 32 px mirror pad changes the answer by more than 0.01 up to 140–200 px from the frame edge for widths 8.5 and 17, so "equal inside the window" has to be defined against the same padding.

**11. Three twin rules.** `read_arms` (5442–5443): within `SAME_ARM` degrees and ring centres within max(width) + `RING_MARGIN`; section 37's "Edges": within `SAME_ARM` and "closer than a width"; section 37's rules: "within the sum of their half-widths for longer than one field length". For two capillaries of half-width 1.5 these are 5.6, 3.6 and 3 px. Pick one; `read_arms`' is the one the readings use.

**12. The T rule and splitting a body.** With `to_forks = True`, `ridge_lines` ends every piece at the fork pixel (6954–6965), so no initial spline has a body for an endpoint to land on; the T rule is reached only by moves, which is fine. Undefined is what a split does to the body's κ windows, its lumen (the whole vessel of `join_pieces`) and its confidence terms: say that both halves inherit them and the lumen is re-read. A fork of three pieces is three endpoints within `RIDGE_JOIN`, which the text calls "a join" (two arms); say "k endpoints within `RIDGE_JOIN` are one vertex of k arms". Two bodies crossing twice within a core fuse by the core rule; outside one they are two crossings and a multi-edge, which the text allows. Verified: the fitted splines' ends lie 0.16 px (median) and 0.63 px (max) from their fork pixel on seed 1, so `RIDGE_JOIN` = 2 reaches every fork.

**13. The render must go through section 19 before section 20.** The hypercolumns take the *contrast* (3127–3137): density divided by the background fit's local noise map `fit["noise"]` and by `contrast_gains[n]`; "the data's noise normalisation" has to include that map, not only the eight channel constants, or the render's score is off by the noise map's spatial variation.

**14. "A corner is usually a junction the fork detection missed."** No evidence in the notebook either way; the generator's vessels wander 0.012 rad per px (6283) and have no corners, and on a tortuous real vessel a corner is a bend. The rule is harmless if the split makes a two-arm vertex that section 36 calls a bend; the rationale should go.

**15. Small errors in the "what exists" table and Part III.** `found_again` is in section 18 (2206), not 22; `picture_blur` is section 29 (6824), not 31 as section 38 says; the harness's centerline metric is within 2 px (6511), not `REPEAT_WITHIN` = 3, and does not go through `found_again`; `extend_ends` also requires the median score along the extension to reach `T_LOW` / 2 (6800); "56 tests" are 39 of this plan's plus 17 of the package's; `WATCH_EVERY` is not defined anywhere (`Recorder.every` defaults to 5); "0.2 terminations per free end" has no source in the notebook, and the text should say it is a guess.

**16. Gauss–Seidel over overlapping lumens.** Subtracting a thicker lumen's rendered layer is exact under the renderer (densities add across lumens in the −log domain after each transmittance's blur, as `synth.py` 449–453), and cores are excluded, so crossings are safe. A twin or collinear overlap is "not a vertex", so its pixels are in both lumens' windows and the two κ share one density: the pass converges to a split that depends on the order. Read a marked overlap's windows for the pair's line mass, not per spline.

## Verified and found correct

- The renderer's description follows `vascnet.synth.render` (436–453): max within a tree, `exp(−od)`, blur on the transmittance, product across trees, `−log`.
- With the pre-interaction map, the self-consistency number of the exact truth rendered with the data's noise and floor constants is 0.996–0.999 in both directions on seeds 1 and 2, within the tubes 0.991–0.998; the data's own repeatability against a second noise realisation is 0.995–1.0 (`repeated`'s share above chance). The gate of step 4 is reachable.
- Section 20's noise constants transfer to a noiseless render (finding 2a says how); the footprint count 2.5 w² matches the rectified channel's correlation area to within 10–40%; a thin vessel's tails at a 90-degree crossing appear in its own channel two pixels beyond the thick vessel's wall.
- `reading_confidence` is exp(score / tests) × (1 − exp(−margin)) (5962–5964); a NaN test fails a partition (5850); `set_partitions` allows no group of one (5755); `contract_cores` marks spurs and keeps them out of a node's ends (5364, 5380); `read_arm` reads a short edge's outer half (5397–5399); `where_axes_meet` is read on the ring and moved no farther than the core (5903–5918); `join_pieces` joins every through group and every two-end node (7796–7804); `ridge_lines` with `to_forks` puts the fork pixel at both ends of every piece (6954–6965); `fit_spline` fits a line to two points (6995).
- `tests/test_watch.py` and `scripts/watch_flux_demo.py` do what the plan says; `fluxnet.py`'s `report` copies its arrays.
